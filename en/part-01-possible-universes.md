# Opening words of Part 1

This book does not start with the right answer.It begins by temporarily letting go of the familiar premise that the universe must exist in only one way.There is certainly one universe as we observe it, but whether the general principles that describe it should allow for only one realization is another matter.

At the end of these possibilities comes the reality renderer architecture.What lies between a possible state and the local reality that is actually observed.This question will guide all subsequent chapters.

# Chapter 1 Is the universe we know the only correct answer?

When you look up at the night sky, the universe appears as one.The stars and galaxies we observe, the darkness between them, the space through which light moves, and the time in which events follow all form one gigantic reality.We were born within this reality, we think by the rules of this reality, and we have never directly looked outside of this reality.Therefore, the question ‘Why is the universe like this?’ often slips into the belief that ‘Does the universe have to be like this?’

However, observed facts and logical necessity are different.It is an observed fact that our universe has three spatial dimensions and one time dimension.It is also a confirmed fact in this universe that electrons have a constant mass and charge, that the speed of light is constant, and that gravity, electromagnetic force, and strong and weak forces operate.However, the conclusion that this value and structure must be unique in all possible realities does not follow from observations alone.

We often think of natural laws as the constitution of the universe.We imagine that the Constitution came first and that the universe was created according to its articles.But what physics actually offers is a little different.Physics finds repeating relationships between observed phenomena and expresses them in equations.The equation includes symmetry, conservation laws, fields, state space, coupling constants, and boundary conditions.Some terms are determined by structure, but some numbers are determined by experiment.

Therefore, a physical theory is usually not a sentence that describes only one world, but rather defines a space of solutions that satisfy certain conditions.The same equation creates a different history if the initial conditions and boundary conditions are different.Even the structure of the same field produces different particle phenomena if the mass and combination are different.A law is not a single result, but is closer to a grammar in which possible outcomes can exist.

The starting point of this book is simple.The idea is not to assume in advance that real-life grammar allows only one sentence.The universe we live in can be a single sentence that its grammar allows.It's a very long sentence, the only one we've been able to read so far, but it's not the same as the grammar itself.

Here, the term ‘possible universe’ does not immediately mean that another universe actually exists somewhere.Defining possible triangles mathematically does not mean that all triangles can be physically drawn.Likewise, the fact that we can conceive of different coherent models of the universe must be separated from the claim that all of those universes actually coexist.

What we want to ask first is not the number of existences, but the conditions of realization.What kind of structure must there be for it to become a reality?What allows a state physically, what reveals it in observable form, and what creates the joint order of particles, space, and time.This question precedes ‘Are there other universes?’

Then, understanding our universe may not mean proving that the universe is unique.Rather, it can be about establishing a space of possible structures and revealing the place our universe occupies within it.There is no need to prove that there is only one city on Earth in order to place it on a map.What is important is the coordinate system, location, and the structure observed at that location.

From this perspective, our universe does not become less important.On the contrary, it becomes a more accurate research subject.If we place the universe as a unique mystery, all its properties seem inevitable.But by placing it among the possible models, we can isolate which properties come from common laws and which properties come from selected conditions of this universe.

This book pushes that separation to its end.We distinguish between the source of a possible state, the structure that translates it into visible reality, and the result that finally appears as an object with mass, charge, spin, and position.Later on, these three layers will be called SOURCE, RENDERER, and PHENOTYPE.But before defining that name, we must first look at how different the universe could be.

# Chapter 2 The idea that laws and constants can be different

What changes when we say that the universe is changing?The easiest idea is to change the value of a constant.The mass of electrons, the strength of electric charge, the magnitude of gravity, the energy of vacuum, and the bonds between particles have measured values ​​in our universe.However, equations in physical theories often receive these values ​​as input from outside.The form of the expression only allows values, but does not necessarily fix the value to one.

Even a slight change in the constant can change the lifespan of a star, the stability of its atoms, and the path of nuclear reactions.However, there is no need to explain all physics focusing on the ‘values ​​at which life is possible.’What WRRA is interested in is not the selection of a universe that is friendly to humans, but how given values ​​and conditions create a consistent physical phenotype.

A deeper change occurs not in the value of the constant but in the type of state.A model has a certain symmetry, and that symmetry determines the acceptable particle representations and force carriers.Different symmetries can lead to different types of charges and different grammars of interaction.A model without the electrons and quarks we are familiar with can also be thought of mathematically, and a model with the same symmetry but a different mass spectrum is also possible.[Refer to Appendix D ‘Spectrum’]

Dimensions also do not need to be viewed only as a fixed background.The representation of field motions, wave propagation, distance dependence of forces, and rotational groups are dimension-sensitive.As the spatial dimension changes, the possibility of a stable structure also changes.However, there is no reason to immediately conclude here that three-dimensional space is the only possible one.This is because the allowable structure may vary depending on which conditions are important.

In our research, dimension is not simply a concept of counting the number of rooms.It is connected to how many directions independent relationships can be preserved and transmitted and what degrees of freedom the observable kinematics have.Instead of showing all possible directions, the dimensional filter started out as a device that selects and organizes directions that are physically realized under specific conditions.

In this case, the word ‘choice’ does not mean will.Just as we say that a magnet selects iron and a filter selects wavelengths, what we mean is that the structure passes the acceptance criteria and excludes the rest from the visible sector.Choice is not a hand outside the law, but an action within the law.

Even within the same general-purpose structure, different inputs and boundary conditions can produce different realizations.To put it simply, one universe instance can be represented by a combination of source data Ξ, renderer conditions Ω, and parameters and boundary conditions θ.The notation U = E(Ξ, Ω; θ) does not mean that the universe has been calculated with one equation, but is a map to separate what is a common structure and what is an instance input.

*U = E(Ξ, Ω; θ)* (1.1)

This distinction also changes the attitude toward using observational constants.Calibrating parameters with observed values ​​is not a failure of the theory.The Standard Model and General Relativity also use measured inputs.The problem is that the entered values ​​are later called predictions.So this book distinguishes between INPUT, CALIBRATED, DERIVED, and PREDICTED.[Refer to Appendix D ‘Standard Model of Particle Physics’]

The role of a universal model is not to create all numbers from scratch.First, it is necessary to find out what data can be input, whether the inputs do not conflict with each other, what relationships one value enforces between several physical quantities, and what is no longer free after correction.

In this way, the explanatory power of physical theory comes not from the absence of input, but from the compression of structure.Translating a hundred observations into a hundred independent functions adds little to the explanation.On the other hand, even if the same number of inputs are used, if they are placed under one common action and the permitted and prohibited relationships are revealed, a new organizing principle is obtained.

WRRA is an attempt to find this organizing principle.It does not first ask, ‘Why is the natural constant always this value?’Instead, it asks, ‘How does a universe with these values ​​realize mass, charge, spin, and position together in the same local reality?’

# Chapter 3 A different reality

We experience space in three directions.Front and back, left and right, and up and down are independent of each other, and objects move in these three directions.Time determines the order of movement, but it is not experienced in the same round-trip direction as space.This 3+1-dimensional structure is so familiar that it feels like the definition of reality.

But in mathematics, dimensions are much more common.The number of independent coordinates needed to specify a state, the size of the expression space in which the symmetry group operates, and the number of independent directions in which information can move can all be expressed in terms of dimension.The dimension of physics is a broader concept than simple visual direction.

As the dimension changes, the physical relationship also changes.The way the force spreads, the Green's function of the wave, the structure of rotation, the expression of spin and the engineering dimensions of the field change.A simple theory at one dimension may not be renormalizable at another dimension, and some topological structures only appear at certain dimensions.

However, we cannot directly say, ‘Because there are complex substances and observers, only three-dimensional space is possible.’The argument judges possibilities based on what we know about matter and life.We must first calculate whether different stability principles and different types of observability exist in other dimensions.

In Minimal Computing Cosmology, dimension becomes a key variable that distinguishes the size of the entire source from the size of visible reality.Even if the source has more degrees of freedom, the observable kinematics may be a compressed representation of some of them.Conversely, there is no need for the four visible dimensions to be stored as four completed spaces in the source.

WRRA describes the visible kinematics of our universe as D_visible = 4.This is the condition that fixes the currently observed 3+1 dimensional phenotype.However, this result does not extend to the claim that the four dimensions must uniquely emerge from all possible sources.

This difference is important.There is a huge logical gap between ‘our instances have four dimensions’ and ‘every possible instance must have four dimensions’.WRRA models the first sentence and does not target the second sentence.

Whether different renderer conditions can create different visible dimensions, whether the same source allows for multiple dimensional phenotypes, or whether different sources can be compressed into the same 3+1 dimensional reality are all open questions.These open problems are not deficiencies but rather research spaces defined by WRRA.

In this view, dimension becomes a property of embodied kinematics rather than the absolute appearance of the universe.We do not look directly at the dimension of the source itself, but measure the dimensionality operating on the local observable algebra and physical state space.

In the second half of the book we will see what conditions this compression must have in order not to lose its positivity.It is enough to remember one thing here.The four dimensions are an important characteristic of our universe that WRRA must explain, but they are not a unique proposition that closes off all possible realities.

# Chapter 4 A universe with different combinations of particles and forces

If you write down a list of what constitutes reality, the particle table of the Standard Model comes to mind.Quarks and leptons are arranged in three generations, photons, gluons, and W·Z bosons transmit force, and the Higgs field participates in electroweak symmetry breaking and mass creation.This structure has achieved tremendous experimental success.

However, the success of the Standard Model does not mean that its structure is the only logically possible quantum field theory.You can write theories with different gauge families, different material representations, different numbers of generations, and different masses and combinations.Many of them are rejected due to anomalies, negative probability, unstable vacuum, and non-locality, but no single mathematically consistent candidate is known.[Refer to Appendix D ‘Gauge Symmetry’]

Possibilities are not imagination without limits.In relativistic quantum theory, energy must not fall infinitely downward, the probability of physical states must not be negative, and distant local observations must maintain causal order.In a gauge theory, non-physical degrees of freedom and physical states must be distinguished, and the structure of necessary terms must be managed even after quantum correction.[See Appendix D ‘Causality and Delayed Response’]

These conditions reduce the likelihood of a universe, but do not necessarily make it a point.The space of feasible universes may be a model space with constraints, neither unlimited chaos nor the only correct answer.

In WRRA, particles are not defined as small beads attached to a source.Physical particles are the phenotypes allowed by the local observation algebra of the visible sector, the Poincaré expression, the gauge expression, and the spectral structure together.Stable isolated poles are one type of particle, but thresholds, resonances, and gauge invariant complexes are also physically manifest different types.

This definition is advantageous for dealing with other universe possibilities.In some instances, an isolated pole may appear as a stable particle, while in other instances the same source material may appear only as a broad resonance or continuous spectrum.The difference in realization is not simply a replacement of the particle list, but a difference in ‘what is seen as a physical object.’

Force is also not understood solely as a way to draw separate lines between particles.Gauge symmetry, local observable algebras, and coupled structures allow for specific interactions.Choosing different renderers and representations leaves open the possibility that the same source may have different visible binding structures.

Of course, there is still no evidence that this possibility has been realized in actual nature.This book does not create a list of universes without evidence and present it as fact.Instead, we separate step by step the structures allowed by the theory, those that are quantum consistent, and those that are consistent with our observations.

This separation is also necessary for new particle predictions.Adding random peaks to a spectrum and calling them new particles is not a prediction.When the conditions of positivity, symmetry, locality, and closure are simultaneously required, a pole or resonance that cannot be removed must remain.And the mass range and charge/spin/decay path must be fixed with the result not being used for calibration.

The purpose of thinking about possible universes is not to say that anything is possible.Rather, it is to strictly narrow down what is possible.WRRA's open universe view is not the absence of rules, but a structure that allows multiple realizations under common rules.

# Chapter 5 Computable Universe and Realizable Universe

Just because you can write down the solution to an equation does not mean it is a physical universe.Mathematical representability, computability, quantum consistency, physical realizability, and observational adequacy are different layers.When these layers are mixed, an error occurs where a beautiful equation looks like an explanation of nature, or a model that fits well observed values ​​looks like a fundamental theory.

In Minimal Computing Cosmology, ‘computation’ does not mean that an external computer runs the universe.This means that the laws of physics themselves are an inherent process of selecting, transforming, and preserving states.Therefore, there is no need to assume an extra-universe programmer or simulator.

The word minimum does not simply mean the smallest amount of information.The principle is to selectively implement necessary relationships and states without visually duplicating all unnecessary degrees of freedom in order to establish reality.Between the entire source and the reality of observation lies the organization of physical accessibility, not the erasure of information.

*U_possible ⊇ U_consistent ⊇ U_realized ⊇ U_observed* (1.2)

What you need to be most careful about at this time is loss during the compression process.If a positive state changes to a negative probability, different charge sectors are mixed incorrectly, or causality breaks down, the output cannot become a physical reality.So the renderer must be an interface that preserves physical tolerances, not just a data compressor.

WRRA expresses this condition in a positive selection or compression structure.The detailed math will be covered later, but the intuition is simple.Even if we make some parts visible in a larger space of possibilities, the dot product and probability interpretation of the visible states must remain consistent.

There is also a need for dynamics between the computable universe and the realizable universe.Just defining a state for a moment does not create a universe in which events continue.Time evolution must preserve the physical state space, and local observations must have consistent order and causal structure.

It also requires interaction.Constructing the spectrum of a free particle is different from maintaining the interaction theory under quantum correction.A model initially expressed with finite parameters may continue to require new terms after loop correction.This may be a failure of the model as a whole, but more often it is a boundary that sets the acceptable range for effective field theory.

*C_total = C_state + C_relation + C_boundary + C_update* (1.3)

Our study does not conceal this boundary.Some free quantum realizations of Bessel common carriers and positive spectral realizations can be constructed, but radical closure in the full strong sense does not automatically follow.Some realizations created the problem of infinitely continuous charged species, while other realizations of a single interacting spectral field provided a way around the problem.

Distinguishing between failed runtime and surviving runtime is itself a function of WRRA.Architecture is not doomed to one implementation method.There may be multiple runtimes producing the same visible phenotype, and we test which of them survive under interaction and quantum correction.

Therefore, feasibility is not achieved with a single declaration.It must pass through the gates of positivity, symmetry, locality, dynamics, interaction, renormalization, and observation matching in order.This book does not say that all gates have already been closed.Instead, it provides a map showing which gates are closed and which are open.

# Chapter 6 Universes we actually created

In the next step we tried to close the kinematic interface.We confirmed that equivalence between inertial frames, Lorentz covariance, and energy-momentum relationships do not conflict with the dimensional filter output.This step was not to calculate all the masses and bonds of the particles, but to prepare an interface that could be equipped with visible 3+1-dimensional kinematics.

# Chapter 7 Our universe is not special

The sentence ‘Our universe is not special’ does not mean that our universe is insignificant.The research principle is not to treat it as an exception to a special rule.This means that we will not add separate terms just for our universe or grant hidden privileges to general-purpose structures to obtain observed results.

This principle was already used as a central assumption in previous dimensional filter universe models.The question at the time was not ‘Why was only this universe chosen?’The question was how accurately our universe, which is one of the many cosmic structures allowed by the general dimensional filter model, can be expressed.

In our tuned model of the universe, observation constants are treated as observed locations in a generic parameter space.In other words, using constants was not a matter of forcing a miracle unique to our universe into a general-purpose equation, but rather specifying the location of our actual observations within the allowed parameter space.

Let's write this division in symbols.If the model space of universe instances allowed by WRRA is 𝔘, it can be written as 𝔘 = {E_WRRA(Ξ, Ω; θ) : (Ξ, Ω, θ) ∈ 𝒫}.Here, 𝒫 is not a set of random values, but a region that satisfies acceptance conditions such as positivity, causality, and locality.

Our universe lies in one instance: U_ours = E_WRRA(Ξ_ours, Ω_ours; θ_ours).This notation is not a declaration that all the data in our universe has already been calculated.It is a ledger for isolating and auditing the common structures and inputs of our universe.

The general-purpose structure provides relationships and acceptance conditions for source, renderer, and phenotype.Our universe instance has observational anchors such as electron mass, coupling constant, vacuum conditions, boundaries and raw data.Only when this input is not hidden can we see what is derived from the structure and what has been compensated for.

Therefore, proof of uniqueness is not a condition for success in this study.If we force that 3+1 dimensions, a specific gauge group, three generations, and current constants must be unique in all possible realities, we confuse the general model and the instance.

Instead, more productive questions arise.What is the minimal input that consistently constitutes an instance of our universe.What is the relationship that is automatically fixed after input?What observation result determines that the runtime of this instance or WRRA is wrong?

The non-privilege principle does not make verification lax.Rather, it makes it more stringent.No matter what the outcome is, it cannot be avoided by saying, ‘It’s because it’s a different universe.’The instances declared to describe our universe must match observations of our universe.The existence of other possible instances does not reduce this responsibility.

This principle is also distinct from the anthropocentric principle.Instead of explaining that the constants were chosen this way because humans exist, we first describe the physically acceptable structures and observational locations of our universe.Life and observer conditions can be studied later, but do not replace the definition of a general-purpose renderer.

Our universe is not special.However, it is irreplaceable in that it is the only universe that we can actually observe.The model space of the theory should be kept wide open, but verification should come from this single observed instance.This is a way to simultaneously maintain open possibilities and empirical responsibility.

# Chapter 8 Differences between the multiverse and possible universe models

When talking about possible universes, the multiverse immediately comes to mind.However, the two concepts are not the same.A possible universe model is a mathematical statement that certain equations and acceptance conditions can have different solutions or instances.The multiverse is an ontological claim that multiple instances of it actually physically exist.

The mere existence of several possible models does not follow from the actual existence of all models.Just because building codes allow multiple plans does not mean that all buildings are erected.Just because the score allows for multiple performances, it does not mean that all performances sound at the same time.

WRRA first deals with blueprints and playing conditions.It defines what possibilities the source provides, what physical parts the renderer selects and compresses, and what local reality the phenotype represents.This structure alone does not determine whether other sources and renderers actually exist.

This moderation is important.The multiverse may be philosophically attractive, but direct verification is difficult.WRRA's reliance on an untested ontology distracts from its original goal of explaining the physical manifestations of our universe.

*∃M : Consistent(M) ⇏ ∃x : Realized(x, M)* (1.5)

Therefore, this book does not say that ‘all possible universes exist.’He says, ‘We are not limited a priori to having only one way of realizing physically consistent reality.’The former is a declaration of existence, and the latter is an openness of research methods.

Model pluralism actually makes comparison possible.We can test whether stochastic positivity is broken without certain conditions, which symmetric expressions create anomalies and fall out, and which dimensions do not maintain the desired local structure.The other possibility is not a decoration that relativizes our universe, but a control that decomposes the conditions of our universe.

Now, the question is divided into two branches: ‘Why does the universe look like this?’First, what common principles define the feasibility of reality?Second, what input and renderer conditions does our universe have within that allowable space?The first question is directed to a universal WRRA, the second question is directed to a quantum and phenomenological realization of instances of our universe.

Between these two questions lies the central concept of this book.Not all possible states will soon be observed.Between the source and visible reality, a structure is needed to select and translate states while preserving physical tolerances.We call that structure the reality renderer.

The renderer is not an out-of-universe computer, not an observer's consciousness, or a random selector.It is an implicit architecture that jointly realizes the source possibilities as phenotypes with local observation algebras and physical Hilbert spaces, Poincaré and gauge representations.[Refer to Appendix D ‘Hilbert Space’]

The question that started from possibility is here narrowed down to the structure of reality.In the next part, we will follow the lineage of minimum computation and dimension filters and look at why a layer called renderer is needed between source and phenotype.

We are not looking for a unique blueprint for the universe.We are looking for the conditions that possible realities must share in order to become reality, and asking how our universe appears in a concrete form under those conditions.The possibilities don't end here.Now we are finally starting to get structure.
