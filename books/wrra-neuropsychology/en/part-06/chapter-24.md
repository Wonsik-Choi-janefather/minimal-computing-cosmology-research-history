# Chapter 24 Stress, Fatigue, Recovery, and Available Resources

### 1 Scene and question

Bodily responses may help meet emergencies, while prolonged demands introduce other burdens. McEwen discussed both protective and damaging effects of stress mediators. (McEwen, 1998 [R12](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJM199801153380307)) Stress is therefore more than a quantity of bad stimulation.

Chapter question: How can fatigue and recovery be specified without one resource score?

### 2 Starting points in established research

#### 2.1 Demands and controllability

Having much to do differs from appraising those tasks as difficult to manage. Control, predictability, and support may change the meaning of identical demands. (Spielman et al., 2020, §14.1 [R1](https://openstax.org/books/psychology-2e/pages/14-1-what-is-stress))

#### 2.2 Dimensions of fatigue

Sleepiness, mental fatigue, and muscle fatigue are different indicators. Questionnaires, tasks, and physiological measurements may read different things. Sleep or activity records can better differentiate current conditions. (NINDS, n.d. [R8](https://www.ninds.nih.gov/sites/default/files/2025-05/understanding-sleep.pdf))

#### 2.3 Recovery paths

Improvement after rest may combine elapsed time, reduced new input, physical recovery, and changed expectations. Comparison with other activities over the same period helps narrow pathways. Recovery rates may differ across people and functions; not all residues receive one decay rate. Chapter 41's single ratio is a small comparison example.

### 4 WRRA-specific transformation

Transformation specification: separate load, residue, and recovery conditions.

WRRA separates demand magnitude, interpretation, coping means, remaining influence, and recovery conditions. Sleep duration, subjective fatigue, and physiological data measure different things. Combining them into one resource value requires explaining weights and units.

#### 4.1 Conditions reducing load

Reducing environmental demands or increasing authority and support differs from regulating individual responses, though both can be combined. The ledger distinguishes externally reduced costs from internally changed states. Improved outcomes count within their scope; further measurement assesses which connection was established.

#### 4.2 Demands and controllability — WRRA interpretation

External demands, personal interpretation, and available responses are recorded separately. Discomfort alone is not a reason to assume the environment is safer than it actually is.

#### 4.3 Dimensions of fatigue — WRRA interpretation

Resource indicators are selected for the purpose; combining indicators requires grounds for their units and weights.

### 5 Outputs and execution record

The current output is an explanatory specification of phenomena, variables, and validation paths. Planned outputs: functional changes and recovery paths.

### 6 Falsification conditions and interpretive limits

Falsification condition: deny explanatory credit if resource indicators are not defined independently of outcomes.

Available resources are not defined retrospectively from behavior. Independently measured conditions must explain subsequent performance. Chapter 42's dimensionless resource values are hypothetical inputs illustrating a principle, not measurements of actual fatigue. Recovery research also separates mere elapsed time from actual environmental change.
