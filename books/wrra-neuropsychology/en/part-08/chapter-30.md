# Chapter 30 Social Perception, Attribution, Attitudes, and Persuasion

### 1 Scene and question

A delayed reply may suggest indifference, yet the other person may be busy or disconnected. Social perception interprets people and situations from limited information; attribution asks where behavior's causes are located. (Spielman et al., 2020, §12.1 [R1](https://openstax.org/books/psychology-2e/pages/12-1-what-is-social-psychology))

Chapter question: How can the other's actual state differ from the observer's interpretation?

### 2 Starting points in established research

#### 2.1 Locating causes

Explaining behavior through personal characteristics or circumstances depends on information and observational perspective. Studies separately present actor and situational information and examine changed judgments. (Spielman et al., 2020, §12.1 [R1](https://openstax.org/books/psychology-2e/pages/12-1-what-is-social-psychology))

#### 2.2 Connecting attitudes and behavior

Reported agreement and actual action may pass through different conditions, including opportunity, cost, social pressure, and habit. (Ajzen, 1991 [R47](https://doi.org/10.1016/0749-5978(91)90020-T))

#### 2.3 Information and relationships in persuasion

Message evidence, source credibility, attention, understanding, and personal goals can be investigated together. A famous speaker does not make a message accurate. (Spielman et al., 2020, §12.3 [R1](https://openstax.org/books/psychology-2e/pages/12-3-attitudes-and-persuasion))

### 4 WRRA-specific transformation

Transformation specification: distinguish the other's actual conditions from the observer's interpretive model.

WRRA separates the other's actual state from the observer's model of it. Strong observer residue may give ambiguous behavior a particular meaning. Independent data must assess accuracy. The record should not imply direct access to another person's mind.

#### 4.1 Locating causes — WRRA interpretation

Observer residue, present inputs, and causal models are separate. Inferred intentions are not recorded as the other's actual state.

#### 4.2 Connecting attitudes and behavior — WRRA interpretation

Attitude reports, intentions, possible actions, and actual choices remain distinct. Changed reports do not establish all subsequent behavior has changed; persistence and context are checked separately.

#### 4.3 Information and relationships in persuasion — WRRA interpretation

WRRA distinguishes which judgments involve information content and relational residue. Persistence with another speaker or after a delay helps assess explanatory scope.

### 5 Outputs and execution record

The current output is an explanatory specification of phenomena, variables, and validation paths. Planned outputs: attributions, attitude reports, and choices.

### 6 Falsification conditions and interpretive limits

Falsification condition: changed responses alone do not establish changed behavior or lasting beliefs.

Persuasion research distinguishes messages, speakers, prior knowledge, and social conditions. Agreement and later choices may differ. WRRA's contribution is tracking which combinations of input and residue alter which expression paths.

#### 6.1 Research on prejudice and behavior

Generalized beliefs, evaluations, and actual discriminatory actions are related but not identical indicators. Differences between explicit responses and automatic tasks also require interpreting the tasks. WRRA separates entry points of group cues, personal experience, and paths by which rules and authority change outcomes. Morally judging a person from one test exceeds its measurement scope.
