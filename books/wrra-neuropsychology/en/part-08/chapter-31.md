# Chapter 31 Attachment, Intimacy, Family, and Interpersonal Relationships

### 1 Scene and question

A close person's words may matter more than a stranger's because shared experience enters current expectations. Relationship research does not explain trust, intimacy, conflict, and support through one person's personality alone. (Cohen and Wills, 1985 [R51](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.98.2.310))

Chapter question: How can relationships in which each person's action becomes the other's next input be recorded?

### 2 Starting points in established research

#### 2.1 A relationship is a two-person process

One person's report can assess relationship quality but is not the complete interaction record. Recording both people's expectations, interpretations, and actions can reveal different experiences.

Cohen and Wills distinguished direct support effects from stress-buffering hypotheses. Their review does not imply that every close relationship has the same effect. (Cohen and Wills, 1985 [R51](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.98.2.310))

### 3 Validation inputs

The concepts and observational evidence from existing research are identified in this chapter's references and Appendix E. Planned inputs for the WRRA application: both parties' reports and interaction records. Measurements and experiments without a separate execution record are research plans, not actual data collected for this manuscript.

### 4 WRRA-specific transformation

Transformation specification: combine person-specific residues with inputs given to each other.

WRRA jointly records each person's present state, residue, and interaction. One person's action becomes the other's input, whose response feeds back as input. The same event need not leave identical residue in both.

#### 4.1 Paths of repeated conflict

One hypothesis is that silence is interpreted as rejection, provoking pressure that increases silence. Testing this requires action timing and each person's interpretation. WRRA combines both states and updates, comparing one-way influences and interaction rather than prejudging one person's personality as the cause.

#### 4.2 Support and autonomy

Assistance may have different outcomes depending on content, received meaning, and choice. Practical burden reduction and emotional support are not one scale. WRRA specifies the provided resources and changed boundaries, distinguishing receiving support from restored function when evaluating effects.

#### 4.3 A relationship is a two-person process — WRRA interpretation

My model of the other and the other's report have different owners. Disagreement is investigated by event and context rather than erased through averaging.

### 5 Outputs and execution record

The current output is an explanatory specification of phenomena, variables, and validation paths. Planned outputs: recurring patterns of expectations and actions within relationships.

### 6 Falsification conditions and interpretive limits

Falsification condition: add a joint interaction model if one party's data are insufficient.

Attachment or relationship types may organize tendencies without fixing every relationship into a type. Changes of partner or situation reveal what persists and what changes. Research distinguishes internal individual changes from changed interaction conditions.

#### 6.1 Measuring relationship change

Conflict frequency, recovery time, mutual understanding, and everyday function can be investigated, with target outcomes fixed beforehand. Where safety or autonomy is compromised, conversational skills alone do not explain every problem. WRRA considers actual authority, risks, and external support alongside internal residue.

### 7 Current verdict

Current verdict: INFERRED for the WRRA correspondences and explanatory structure; OPEN for empirical fit of the transformation.

Chapter in one sentence: Relationships require jointly recording two people's states, residues, and feedback.
