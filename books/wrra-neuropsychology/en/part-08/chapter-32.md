# Chapter 32 Conformity, Obedience, Groups, Cooperation, and Conflict

### 1 Scene and question

Choices may differ between judging alone and speaking before a group. Majority information, belonging motives, authority, and sanctions may each matter. This is why conformity and obedience need not share one cause. (Spielman et al., 2020, §12.4 [R1](https://openstax.org/books/psychology-2e/pages/12-4-conformity-compliance-and-obedience))

Chapter question: How do group rules and information flows change individual choices and collective outcomes?

### 2 Starting points in established research

#### 2.1 Following information or norms?

Changing judgment because others seem better informed differs from changing a public response to preserve belonging. Separating private and public judgments and manipulating information reliability helps investigate these paths. (Spielman et al., 2020, §12.4 [R1](https://openstax.org/books/psychology-2e/pages/12-4-conformity-compliance-and-obedience))

#### 2.2 Authority and responsibility

Explaining obedience requires examining authority's basis, refusal costs, and responsibility allocation. Institutional boundaries may intervene between moral judgment and actual action. (Spielman et al., 2020, §12.4 [R1](https://openstax.org/books/psychology-2e/pages/12-4-conformity-compliance-and-obedience))

#### 2.3 Cooperation and shared resources

Individual gains may conflict with collective maintenance when resources are shared. Information disclosure, repeated meetings, sanctions, and trust can be varied to study cooperation. (Ostrom, 2009 [R48](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1172133))

### 4 WRRA-specific transformation

Transformation specification: update authority, connection structure, and individual states together.

WRRA specifies group rules and information flows, personal goals and resources, and boundaries of refusal or exit. Cooperation prompts investigation of repeated interaction, responsibility, and disclosure as well as personality. Persistent conflict also requires recording expectations about one another's responses.

#### 4.1 Explaining collective outcomes

Groups of equally capable people may differ because of connections, speaking order, and information sharing. WRRA compares sums of individual states with interaction effects. Adding a network does not itself explain a group phenomenon: the model must compute or the study must measure how outcomes change when particular connections change.

#### 4.2 Following information or norms? — WRRA interpretation

WRRA specifies which outputs involve shared inputs, social boundaries, and individual goals.

#### 4.3 Authority and responsibility — WRRA interpretation

#### 4.4 Cooperation and shared resources — WRRA interpretation

### 6 Falsification conditions and interpretive limits

Falsification condition: reconsider the proposed connection mechanism if structural changes have no effect.

Whether individual-level means fully explain collective outcomes must be checked. A few network positions or decision rules may substantially alter results. Without neural measurements, such cases explain social state transitions, not computed neural mechanisms.
