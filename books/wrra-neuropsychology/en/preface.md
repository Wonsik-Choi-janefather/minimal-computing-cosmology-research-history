# Preface

This book begins with a question. Can WRRA connect the ways different disciplines explain the same phenomenon, and generate meaningful explanations and computations through those connections? We chose psychology and neuroscience as the first case for exploring this possibility.

Here, integration across disciplines means connecting explanations from fields with different objects of study and units of measurement. We preserve the respective meanings of experience reports, behavior, neural data, and bodily data while specifying their relationships through states, transformations, and observation rules. We distinguish descriptions rewritten in a common vocabulary from connections that actually enter an explanation or computation.

The book is for readers interested in mind and behavior and researchers exploring connections between disciplines. It starts with familiar phenomena—memory and learning, emotion and relationships—examines what each field has established, and asks what connecting them through WRRA can additionally explain. The familiar topical organization of general psychology provides a guide to this inquiry.

## WRRA's purpose and starting point

WRRA stands for Wonsik Reality-Renderer Architecture. It is a framework for explaining a single, nonprivileged universe through the common method of minimal computation (termed Minimum Computation in Core 1.0), common carriers, and phenotypes. This book examines whether that method is also useful for connecting phenomena studied by different disciplines. The explanation below draws on Wonsik Choi's WRRA Core 1.0 and Wonsik Choi and Jeongin Choi's *How Life Runs*. Appendix A presents the standard definitions. (Choi, 2026 [W1](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22650956); Choi and Choi, 2026 [W2](https://github.com/Wonsik-Choi-janefather/how-life-runs-v2.7.3))

WRRA asks not only what result occurred but also what it received as input and through which relationships and rules it was executed. Distinguishing input, present state, the influence of previous experience, transmission and updating, rendering and observation allows us to specify where disciplinary explanations connect and where links are missing. Applications in particular fields make this execution structure concrete for the object being studied.

## Minimal computation is a criterion for preserving required function

Minimal computation first specifies the functions to preserve and the errors that can be tolerated, then uses the states and processing needed to meet those criteria. A reduction in time that sacrifices accuracy or safety does not count as performing the same function at lower cost. The margin and redundancy needed to preserve function also enter the cost accounting. What was reduced and what was preserved must both be recorded.

## A common carrier asks what is shared

A common carrier is a transmission or updating structure reused for distinguishable information or operations. Even where the underlying infrastructure is shared, information content, ownership, transmission paths, and purposes remain distinct. Networks reused by several functions are possible candidates in neuropsychology. What is actually shared requires investigation; the term common carrier alone does not identify a particular brain region or neurotransmitter.

## Distinguishing phenotype from observable

A phenotype is an implemented result produced from an internal state through execution and rendering rules under boundary conditions. An observable is a value read from that result using an instrument and a procedure. This distinction separates behavior generated by a model from behavior actually recorded, and a person's reported experience from a signal measured by a researcher. Rules must connect what was observed to the internal state inferred from it.

## Connecting present state and residue through execution structure

Present state comprises the conditions involved in execution now. Residue is the part of a previous event's influence that remains active in the present; it can include learned skills, familiarity, and trust. The past enters execution through present states, residues, or accessible records. A model that reads records must specify their present location and access paths. Residue may be part of present state, so the definitions must prevent counting the same information twice.

A concrete application specifies inputs and their sources, then states how relationships and rules change the state. Boundaries define the system's scope and permissible flows. Updating rules determine the next state; rendering and observation rules determine what can be read from it. The ledger records ownership, units, times, sources, and transformations. Actual structures may contain feedback and parallel processing: phenomena are not assumed to pass just once through a single linear sequence.

## Reading one hypothetical scene

Consider a hypothetical study of a person who stops speaking just before a presentation. The current question and the audience's reactions are candidate inputs. Fatigue and task understanding are candidate present conditions. The influence remaining from previous presentations is a candidate residue; speaking time and authority are boundaries of the task and relationships. This is an illustration of constructing research variables, not a finding that establishes the cause of silence.

A WRRA application specifies how to measure these candidates and the rules connecting them, then explains or computes how changes in conditions affect the timing or choice of speaking. Connecting psychological reports and behavioral data to bodily and neural data also requires connecting measurement times and errors. If a neural pathway corresponding to a common carrier has not been measured, that pathway remains a question for subsequent research. Reproduction of a hypothetical computation and fit to actual people receive separate verdicts.

In this book, neuroscience and brain science refer to overlapping fields. The research focus is on connections between psychology and neuroscience that actually contribute to explanation and computation. Readers can apply these questions to the chapters on memory and learning, emotion and relationships.

## Criteria for assessing integration's contribution

Claims are assessed in the order validation inputs → WRRA-specific transformation → output → falsification condition. We identify the evidence from each field, the structures and rules by which WRRA connects it, what the connection explains or computes, and the results that would require revising it. An external theory is recorded as integrated only to the extent that it is actually connected to and executed in computations within WRRA.

Calibration using verified observations and reproduction of known values through the framework's own rules count as legitimate construction, reality consistency, and explanatory performance. Originality is assessed in the structure of connections, transformations, and explanations. An unmeasured quantity generated by a fixed model is recorded as that model's prediction. Independent prediction is not a mandatory condition of integration's contribution or originality.

## What this book provides

Chapters 1–39 cover existing research and its proposed WRRA correspondences. Chapter 40 onward examines model construction, computation, and the conditions for integration. The hypothetical-input computations in Chapters 41 and 42 can be reproduced using the code in Appendix D. The conceptual connections and hypothetical computations presented here establish the basis for identifying subsequent tasks involving actual behavioral and neural data.

Each chapter follows eight stages: scene and question; starting points in established research; validation inputs; WRRA-specific transformation; outputs and execution record; falsification conditions and interpretive limits; current verdict; and next questions and evidence. Author–year citations point to existing research; WRRA interpretations and computational records identify the author's work. Everyday scenes are hypothetical illustrations unless presented as experimental examples with sources. References are in Appendix E and the common writing contract in Appendix F.

Readers who want to follow a connection from beginning to end may first read the speaking example in Section 4.2 of Chapter 45. It shows how the scene in Chapter 1 connects to the hypothetical computation in Chapter 42 and where links remain missing.

## Common rules for reading chapter specifications

Author–year citations refer to the existing research in the immediately preceding sentence or paragraph. Subsequent WRRA transformations, hypothetical scenes, and proposed studies are the author's work. Support for existing research does not substitute for empirical validation of the WRRA application. This rule applies throughout the book.

The planned inputs in Section 3 and planned outputs in Section 5 are specifications for future investigation. Without a separate execution record, they should not be read as results collected, analyzed, or executed for this manuscript. The terminology correspondences in Section 4 are a starting point for constructing execution rules; they do not by themselves establish completed computation or integration of neural mechanisms.

Empirical tests in Section 6 whose tolerances have not been specified are OPEN. When such tests are undertaken, comparison criteria must be fixed before examining results, in light of measurement error and the research purpose. INFERRED in Section 7 marks a proposed explanatory structure; OPEN marks unresolved empirical fit. Appendix F explains the verdicts and writing principles in detail.

Reference identifiers in Section 8 lead to the bibliographic details, DOIs, and URLs in Appendix E. Core definitions are in Appendix A; numerical results and reproduction code are in Appendix D. Shared explanations are consolidated here, leaving each chapter's particular inputs, transformations, outputs, and falsification conditions in place.

## Scope of This Revision and the Reason for Core 2.1

This English v0.10 translates the Korean consciousness extension and integrates it with the full earlier English manuscript: twelve parts and 54 chapters. Nine new chapters, 45–53, precede the former synthesis chapter, now Chapter 54. Part 11 connects neuropsychology and consciousness research. Part 12 examines extension questions, distinguishing testable subclaims from philosophical interpretation. Animal and artificial-system consciousness connect with comparative cognition and computational research; cosmological inevitability, souls, and reincarnation are not presented as established neuropsychological knowledge.

Core 2.1 is applied because investigating maintenance, amendment, and inheritance requires separating present outputs from the organization generating them. Its source is Wonsik Choi and Jeongin Choi's WRRA Core 2.1 (W3); the consciousness specifications are this book's domain application. Earlier Chapters 1–44 originated under Core 1.0. Chapters 41–42 and Appendix D retain their original calculations and verification scope. Appendix A preserves the earlier explanation and adds A12 to map the extension. New chapters are not assigned fresh computational or empirical validation.

Original theories and findings are credited in W3–W5 and R71–R89. The English translation preserves the distinctions among experience, observable report, functional reproduction, structural inheritance, and continuity of the same subject. This manuscript's assembly precedes online publication; it does not itself certify publication or a new external review.
