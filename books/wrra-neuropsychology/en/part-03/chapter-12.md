# Chapter 12 Consciousness, Nonconscious Processing, Sleep, and Dreams

### 1 Scene and question

We cannot directly report every processing operation. Evidence that information influenced behavior and a claim that it was consciously experienced require different tests. Distinguishing consciousness, attention, and reporting ability is a starting point. (Spielman et al., 2020, §4.1 [R1](https://openstax.org/books/psychology-2e/pages/4-1-what-is-consciousness))

Chapter question: How can processing, conscious experience, and later reporting be distinguished?

### 2 Starting points in established research

Sleep involves several physiological states rather than simply suspending wakefulness. Relationships among sleep, memory, and everyday functioning are important neuroscience topics. (NINDS, n.d. [R8](https://www.ninds.nih.gov/sites/default/files/2025-05/understanding-sleep.pdf))

#### 2.1 Consciousness and reporting ability

A stimulus influencing behavior does not establish awareness without separate measurement. Conversely, failure to report does not easily establish absence of experience.

#### 2.2 Observations distinguishing sleep states

Sleep research distinguishes states through indicators such as EEG, eye movements, and muscle activity. Biological rhythms and time spent awake are investigated alongside them. (Spielman et al., 2020, §4.3 [R1](https://openstax.org/books/psychology-2e/pages/4-3-stages-of-sleep))

#### 2.3 Dreams and the timing of recall

Questions about dreams read post-awakening reports rather than directly accessing the experience. When someone was awakened, the delay before questioning, and question wording all enter the observation. Numbers or meanings of unremembered dreams are not supplied without evidence.

### 4 WRRA-specific transformation

Transformation specification: separate state changes, memory accessibility, and reporting paths.

#### 4.1 From existing research to a WRRA application

WRRA can compare present-state and residue changes before and after sleep. This book does not assume a single rule according to which sleep deletes all unnecessary memories.

#### 4.2 Consciousness and reporting ability — WRRA interpretation

WRRA distinguishes stimulus processing, accessibility, and response rules. Preserving the distinction between philosophical questions about consciousness and task-measured access clarifies the scope of findings.

#### 4.3 Observations distinguishing sleep states — WRRA interpretation

Before–after sleep changes do not alone establish causes; elapsed time, new experiences, and testing times are compared. In data using real time units, model update intervals are distinguished from arbitrary iteration counts.

#### 4.4 Dreams and the timing of recall — WRRA interpretation

WRRA treats candidate dream experience, currently accessible traces, and rendered reports as separate layers.

### 5 Outputs and execution record

The current output is an explanatory specification of phenomena, variables, and validation paths. Planned output: differences in performance and experience reports across states.

### 6 Falsification conditions and interpretive limits

Falsification condition: restrict an interpretation as a difference in consciousness itself if reporting ability explains the results.

Dream reports also have observation paths. The time of experience is distinguished from recall after awakening, and unreported is not automatically judged unexperienced. This book does not claim to solve the nature of consciousness. It begins with what can be computed about information leading to reports and behavior under specified states.

#### 6.1 Researchable questions and remaining problems

Even without resolving consciousness's nature, we can study which information is used in reports and behavior under different conditions. The same tasks can be presented across states, accuracy and confidence compared, and possible neural indicators connected. These are partial achievements in consciousness research. The extent actually computed in an explanation connecting neural signals and subjective experience is stated separately.
