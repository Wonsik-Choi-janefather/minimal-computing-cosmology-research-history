# Chapter 8 Heredity, Development, Experience, and Neuroplasticity

### 1 Scene and question

People begin with biological conditions preceding experience, but these do not uniquely specify every later response. Genetic differences, developmental environments, learning, and current situations must be considered together. Population-level relationships must not be converted directly into an individual's destiny. (Spielman et al., 2020, §3.1 [R1](https://openstax.org/books/psychology-2e/pages/3-1-human-genetics))

Chapter question: How can genetic and experiential influences be connected along developmental time?

### 2 Starting points in established research

#### 2.1 Genes and expression

Genetic information manifests in function through expression, proteins, cells, and connections. In the nervous system, when and where a gene acts matters. (NINDS, n.d. [R27](https://www.ninds.nih.gov/health-information/patient-caregiver-education/brain-basics-genes-work-brain))

#### 2.2 Not translating heritability into an individual percentage

Heritability concerns statistical decomposition of variation in a particular population and environment. It does not mean that a stated percentage of an individual's characteristic was genetically produced. Estimates may change with the distribution of environments. (Spielman et al., 2020, §3.1 [R1](https://openstax.org/books/psychology-2e/pages/3-1-human-genetics))

#### 2.3 Experience timing and conditions for change

Similar experiences can have different outcomes with different timing, repetition, rest, and context. A sensitive period is a research concept concerning conditions of heightened influence from particular inputs; it does not establish complete impossibility of change at other times. (Spielman et al., 2020, §9.3 [R1](https://openstax.org/books/psychology-2e/pages/9-3-stages-of-development))

### 4 WRRA-specific transformation

Transformation specification: distinguish initial conditions from experience-dependent updating.

WRRA separates starting conditions and subsequent updates. Initial connections and bodily conditions constrain possible changes; actual experience changes which paths develop within those possibilities. Neuroplasticity is important here, but not every change is beneficial or equally possible at every time.

#### 4.1 Explaining plasticity and stability together

If every experience greatly changed every connection, learned functions would be hard to maintain. With no change, adaptation to new environments would be difficult. WRRA addresses this by separately specifying maintenance and updating conditions. Comparing persistent traces with rapidly changing responses helps determine the number of required states and their timescales.

#### 4.2 Genes and expression — WRRA interpretation

WRRA separates inherited information from current execution conditions. Discovering a genetic association and computing a path to behavior are different achievements. Measurement and modeling must fill the connection between them.

#### 4.3 Not translating heritability into an individual percentage — WRRA interpretation

Distinguishing initial conditions from subsequent updating also requires keeping population-level variance separate from an individual's actual history.

### 6 Falsification conditions and interpretive limits

Falsification condition: add condition dependence if the same coefficients do not hold in different environments.

Research separately records genetic information, developmental timing, experience type and intensity, and observation intervals. Persistence of residue differs from difficulty changing it. Examining whether an old response changes in a new environment can allow maintenance and updating rules to be estimated separately.
