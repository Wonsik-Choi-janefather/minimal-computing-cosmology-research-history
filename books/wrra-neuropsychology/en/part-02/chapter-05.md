# Chapter 5 Neurons, Synapses, and Neural Signal Transmission

### 1 Scene and question

Many transmission processes occur between hearing speech and moving a hand. Electrical activity and chemical and electrical interactions between cells provide their physical basis. Cell types, connection direction, and temporal activity patterns must be examined together. Glia and the surrounding environment also belong within the research scope. (Spielman et al., 2020, §3.2 [R1](https://openstax.org/books/psychology-2e/pages/3-2-cells-of-the-nervous-system))

Chapter question: How do momentary neural signals differ from lasting changes in connections?

### 2 Starting points in established research

#### 2.1 Electrical state changes and action potentials

Ion distributions differ across neuronal membranes; channels and transport processes influence membrane potential. Action potentials are rapid changes involving voltage-dependent channels. Stronger stimuli do not make individual action potentials increase in proportion to the intensity of a mental experience. Timing, frequency, and which cells are active together must also be considered. (Spielman et al., 2020, §3.2 [R1](https://openstax.org/books/psychology-2e/pages/3-2-cells-of-the-nervous-system))

#### 2.2 What changes at a synapse

At chemical synapses, released neurotransmitters affect the next cell through receptors and subsequent processes. Effects depend on receptors, cell state, and connection location as well as the transmitter's name. Excitation and inhibition operate together in circuits; they are not simply good and bad signals. (Spielman et al., 2020, §3.2 [R1](https://openstax.org/books/psychology-2e/pages/3-2-cells-of-the-nervous-system))

#### 2.3 Fast signals and lasting changes

An episode of activity and subsequent changes in connection properties occur on different timescales. Molecular and synaptic memory research provides concrete candidates for changes persisting after learning. (Kandel, 2001 [R26](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1067020))

#### 2.4 Animal experiments on connection changes

Bliss and Lømo stimulated the perforant path in anesthetized rabbits and recorded synaptic responses in the dentate gyrus. They observed a lasting increase in transmission responses after strong stimulation. What was directly measured was transmission in a particular pathway, not recall of memory content or human experience reports. (Bliss and Lømo, 1973 [R4](https://doi.org/10.1113/jphysiol.1973.sp010273))

Scope of evidence: lasting neural changes can be proposed as candidates for implementing residue. Whether a candidate explains the behavioral effects of particular learning requires separate input and observation rules.

### 4 WRRA-specific transformation

Transformation specification: distinguish shared transmission paths from channel-specific effects.

A WRRA common carrier is not defined by naming a single substance. A transmission network or updating structure reused by several functions can be a concrete candidate. Sharing a structure does not mean every signal has the same meaning. What is transmitted, through which path, and which state it affects must be differentiated.

#### 4.1 Electrical state changes and action potentials — WRRA interpretation

WRRA defines states while distinguishing the magnitude of a single value from an activity pattern.

#### 4.2 What changes at a synapse — WRRA interpretation

WRRA transmission rules specify the source and destination cells and the relevant state dependence.

#### 4.3 Fast signals and lasting changes — WRRA interpretation

Fast activity can be treated as part of present state and maintained properties affecting later trials as candidate residues. Their actual contribution to behavior must be connected; persistence alone does not identify them as the owner of a particular memory.

### 6 Falsification conditions and interpretive limits

Falsification condition: investigate another implementation if the effect persists when the proposed pathway is controlled.

Classic demonstrations of persistent changes in synaptic transmission support changes in connection properties after experience. (Bliss and Lømo, 1973 [R4](https://doi.org/10.1113/jphysiol.1973.sp010273)) Identifying a particular transmission change as the content of a particular memory requires further evidence. WRRA residue provides a question for locating that connection, not permission to call all neural changes memory.

#### 6.1 A research example and its specification

Suppose neural responses and behavior differ before and after repeated stimulation. Conditions must distinguish adaptation, changes in sensory reception, motor fatigue, and learning. Candidate transmission rules can be tested by changing stimulation location or intervals. This chapter does not execute a new neuron model; it provides design criteria for a concrete model connecting cells and behavior.
