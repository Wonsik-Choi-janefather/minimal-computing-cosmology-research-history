# Chapter 7 The Body, Endocrine System, and Interoception

### 1 Scene and question

Hunger or a racing heart can change how the same situation is experienced. The body is more than a background to psychological research. Interoception involves detecting and interpreting signals inside the body; autonomic and endocrine changes provide research questions linking mind and body. (Garfinkel et al., 2015 [R36](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.biopsycho.2014.11.004); Spielman et al., 2020, §3.5 [R1](https://openstax.org/books/psychology-2e/pages/3-5-the-endocrine-system))

Chapter question: How do bodily changes and interpretations of them jointly influence experience?

### 2 Starting points in established research

#### 2.1 Timescales in the nervous and endocrine systems

Neural transmission and hormonal regulation influence bodily states across several timescales. Autonomic activity may differ by situation and organ. Without knowing which indicators respond quickly or slowly, measurement order can be mistaken for causal order. (Spielman et al., 2020, §3.5 [R1](https://openstax.org/books/psychology-2e/pages/3-5-the-endocrine-system))

#### 2.2 Interoception and interpretation

Interoceptive performance accuracy, the subjective tendency to consider oneself sensitive to bodily signals, and metacognitive awareness of agreement between performance and confidence can be measured separately. Garfinkel and colleagues investigated relationships among these dimensions using studies including heartbeat tasks. The proposal to measure detection and threat interpretation separately is this book's research design. (Garfinkel et al., 2015 [R36](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.biopsycho.2014.11.004))

#### 2.3 Approaching the experience of pain

Pain experience relates to signals concerning damage, but observed damage alone cannot replace all reported suffering. Research considers bodily state, attention, expectations, and context together. Self-report is central evidence about a person's experience and is not invalidated merely by differing from a physiological indicator. (Raja et al., 2020 [R37](https://doi.org/10.1097/j.pain.0000000000001939))

### 3 Validation inputs

The concepts and observational evidence from existing research are identified in this chapter's references and Appendix E. Planned inputs for the WRRA application: physiological data such as heart rate, subjective experience, and context. Measurements and experiments without a separate execution record are research plans, not actual data collected for this manuscript.

#### 3.1 Separating measurement conditions

Similar heart rates immediately after exercise and before an evaluation may occur in different contexts and behaviors. Alternative conditions should be compared rather than inferring an emotional cause from a bodily indicator alone. Repeated measurements tracing which reports and behaviors follow bodily changes can investigate interactions between current inputs and residue. This chapter provides no new diagnostic accuracy estimates for clinical biomarkers.

### 4 WRRA-specific transformation

Transformation specification: separate bodily inputs, interpretation, and behavioral paths.

WRRA distinguishes input from the body from its interpretation. Heart-rate changes and anxiety reports are different variables even when they co-occur. Exercise, pain, medication, and situational interpretations must be examined. Shortcuts such as judging personality or intention from one hormone level are avoided.

#### 4.1 Timescales in the nervous and endocrine systems — WRRA interpretation

WRRA separately records input time, bodily response time, and report time.

#### 4.2 Approaching the experience of pain — WRRA interpretation

WRRA's phenotype connection respects experience reports while specifying their measurement paths.

### 5 Outputs and execution record

The current output is an explanatory specification of phenomena, variables, and validation paths. Planned output: correspondences between bodily responses and experience across conditions.

### 6 Falsification conditions and interpretive limits

Falsification condition: narrow the proposed mediation path if physiological changes do not explain the psychological outcome.

Including bodily state makes questions about individual responses more concrete. Where do current fatigue and earlier experience each act? Does changing bodily conditions change responses? If not, other residue or relational conditions can be investigated. This chapter proposes possible connections without establishing any biomarker as a dedicated WRRA indicator.

Chapter in one sentence: Tracing experiential differences requires distinguishing bodily state from the conditions under which it is interpreted.
