# Appendix F Common Writing Contract for the WRRA Academic Series

## F1 Purpose of the common writing contract

The WRRA series starts from established research in each discipline and reconstructs phenomena through minimal computation, common carriers, and phenotype. Biology, psychology, physics, and other fields share an order for reading claims and evidence. The aim is a common explanatory and validation contract, not identical numbers of parts and chapters.

This extends the organization of *How Life Runs*: established research, WRRA reconstruction, comparative reading, interpretation–prediction boundaries, and next steps with references. Neuropsychology applies the format below.

## F2 Common book structure

The preface states audience, purpose, and present achievements. The introduction briefly explains WRRA and guides readers through familiar disciplinary classifications and explanatory sequences. Chapters follow F3's eight stages. The final part organizes domain computations, synthesis, and further research.

Shared appendices include standard Core 1.0 explanation, terminology correspondences, input and variable specifications, execution records and code, references, and this contract. Core definitions remain distinct from applications; definitions from the same Core version are not arbitrarily altered between volumes.

## F3 Eight stages for every chapter

Sections 1–8 form the common stages. Supplementary headings use parent-section numbers such as 1.1 and 1.2. They are not additional stages; each chapter includes as many as its sections require.

1 Scene and question: begin with an observable phenomenon or concrete problem and delimit the explanatory question. Distinguish factual from hypothetical scenes.

2 Starting points in established research: present established mechanisms, major theories, measurement scope, and open questions. Existing achievements are not rewritten as WRRA discoveries.

3 Validation inputs: distinguish observations, constants, datasets, literature, and hypothetical settings. Record sources, units, timing, errors, and calibration roles. Missing data are labeled acquisition plans.

4 WRRA-specific transformation: specify task-relevant SOURCE, LAW/RELATION, STATE/RESIDUE, BOUNDARY, COMMON CARRIER, UPDATE, RENDERER, and observation paths. Select the structures needed for explanation and computation rather than mechanically adding every term. Equations, code, and actual connections distinguish external citation from internal implementation.

5 Outputs and execution record: record actual numerical, explanatory, and model outputs. Unexecuted outputs are planned outputs. State environment, code, inputs, and tolerances and distinguish empirical from hypothetical data.

6 Falsification conditions and interpretive limits: specify which observations or computations challenge which claims. State limits of interpretation, reproduction, and prediction; distinguish coefficient and structural changes; disclose alternatives and identifiability.

7 Current verdict: judge each claim after presenting inputs, transformations, outputs, and falsification conditions. Do not assign one blanket grade to a whole chapter or discipline. End with one sentence expressing the chapter's point.

8 Next questions and evidence: specify data to obtain and work to execute and connect chapters, references, and DOIs. Disclose absent raw data or code.

## F4 Meaning of claim-level verdicts

EXACT: a relationship mathematically derived or proved under stated assumptions. Numerical agreement alone is insufficient.

EMPIRICAL PASS: a claim supported within specified actual data, comparison criteria, and tolerances. State data scope and calibration roles.

COMPUTATIONAL PASS: successful execution and reproduction under fixed inputs, rules, and checks. Fit to actual phenomena requires separate evidence.

INFERRED: a structure or interpretation proposed from existing evidence without direct verification of that connection or completed computation.

FAIL: failure of the claim or execution check under prespecified conditions. Preserve failure scope and reproduction procedure.

OPEN: a concrete question and verification path remain, with current evidence insufficient to close the conclusion.

NOT ESTABLISHED: required evidence is not established, so the claim cannot yet be asserted. Unperformed research is not success; absence of failure evidence is not automatically FAIL.

These labels are not one ranking. One model may have EXACT mathematical properties, COMPUTATIONAL PASS implementation, and OPEN empirical fit. Verdicts are scoped to individual claims.

## F5 Reproduction, calibration, and prediction as achievements

Revising a model using verified constants and observations is legitimate construction. The ledger records which data determine which rules or coefficients. Reproducing known values through its own structure counts as reality consistency and explanation.

Originality is assessed through WRRA structure, transformations, connections, and explanation, not numerical novelty alone. Independent prediction is not mandatory for theoretical standing or originality. Once a model is fixed using verified values, deriving unmeasured quantities through its own structure counts as that fixed model's WRRA prediction.

Other theories producing equal numbers do not erase calculations actually performed within WRRA. External theories support validation and reinforcement; connections not implemented or computed internally are not marked fully integrated. Comparison models receive the same standards for data, error, and claim scope.

## F6 Validation methods vary by field

Biology and neuropsychology use observation, experiment, and analysis; physics uses measurement, mathematical derivation, and model computation as appropriate. Mathematical claims require assumptions, proofs, and counterexample conditions; social sciences require sampling, measurement, and identification conditions. Fields need not fit one experimental type.

The common format makes evidence comparable. It does not fill missing data with terminology or force different objects and timescales into one numerical scale.

## F7 Claim-ledger fields

Connect claim identifier and statement, target and scope, input sources and versions, roles of constants and coefficients, WRRA transformations and code locations, outputs and errors, falsification conditions, verdicts, revision histories, and next tests. Separate subclaims where evidence types differ.

Neuropsychology example NP41 uses hypothetical initial trace 1 and retention 0.85, updating the next r as 0.85r to obtain 0.196874 after 10 updates. Reexecution outside the table's rounding tolerance fails the implementation check. Its verdict is COMPUTATIONAL PASS; fit to human memory is OPEN.

Example NP42 uses hypothetical failures and resource values, combining residue updates with logistic output to calculate choice probabilities. Latent-scale transformations preserving observed probabilities are also checked. Implementation reproduction, latent identifiability, and fit to human behavior receive separate verdicts.

## F8 Manuscript-completion checks

Check that questions connect to inputs, external evidence differs from WRRA work, and execution records differ from plans. Inputs, transformations, outputs, and falsification conditions precede conclusions. Literature alone does not complete implementation; hypothetical calculations are not observations.

Titles, contents, and cases may vary across disciplines, but stage order and verdict meanings remain common. Record Core, domain-model, and editorial versions separately to track later revisions.

## F9 In-text citations and evidence scope

Place author–year citations and identifiers immediately beside sentences or short paragraphs stating existing concepts or findings. Specify textbook sections, article DOIs, or institutional pages. Do not leave readers to connect claims to a bibliography on their own.

Where established research and author applications share a paragraph, separate the statements and label WRRA interpretation or proposed design. Do not generalize beyond species, samples, tasks, and measurement scope. Reviews and meta-analyses are not individual experiments; institutional guidance is not evidence establishing a particular intervention's effect.

Hypothetical inputs, computations, and reproduction code are distinguished from external experimental data. Additional citations do not automatically change empirical verdicts. Unsupported connections are narrowed or recorded as hypotheses and subsequent work.
