# Chapter 38 Psychotherapy, Biological Treatment, and Environmental Intervention

### 1 Scene and question

Psychotherapy includes several approaches to emotional, cognitive, and behavioral difficulties. Type and suitability depend on the problem and personal circumstances, and may complement medication or other treatments. Effects are assessed through outcomes such as symptoms, function, and quality of life. (NIMH, n.d. [R20](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/topics/psychotherapies))

Chapter question: How does evidence of treatment effects differ from evidence for their mechanisms?

### 2 Starting points in established research

#### 2.1 Locations of change in psychotherapy

Cognitive-behavioral and relational or interpersonal approaches have different questions and procedures. Evidence must be checked for each target problem and method. (NIMH, n.d. [R20](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/topics/psychotherapies))

#### 2.2 Biological treatments and paths

Medication, brain stimulation, and related interventions are clinical domains with specific indications, evidence, and risks. This book provides no new prescription or procedure-selection criteria. (Spielman et al., 2020, §16.2 [R1](https://openstax.org/books/psychology-2e/pages/16-2-types-of-treatment))

#### 2.3 Effects of environments and services

Housing, financial support, and work or school adjustments can change people's circumstances. When combined with psychological intervention, their contributions should be investigated separately.

#### 2.4 Treatment effects require specified comparators

Cuijpers and colleagues synthesized 409 trials with 52,702 participants on CBT for depression. Outcomes were better than controls such as usual care or waiting lists, while differences from other psychotherapies were small and nonsignificant in most sensitivity analyses. Interpretation depends on what was compared and when. (Cuijpers et al., 2023 [R64](https://doi.org/10.1002/wps.21069))

Scope of evidence: data assessing a treatment differ from data assessing WRRA connections. Explanatory paths can be compared when intervention locations, intermediate changes, and final function are actually measured.

### 4 WRRA-specific transformation

Transformation specification: distinguish intervention location, mediation paths, and outcomes.

WRRA provides a framework for recording where intervention acts. Psychological interventions may change interpretations and behavioral rules, environmental interventions may alter choices and burdens, and biological treatments may change bodily or neural processes. Each actual mechanism requires its own evidence; this is not a fixed one-to-one mapping for every treatment.

Treatment effectiveness and correctness of a WRRA explanation require separate evidence. Improved outcomes do not establish the expected mediator. Any new WRRA-based intervention receives the same effect and risk criteria as existing treatments. This book announces no new treatment efficacy.

#### 4.1 Locations of change in psychotherapy — WRRA interpretation

WRRA records intended changes to interpretation, action rules, relational inputs, or present resources. Identically named treatments still require records of actual content and dose.

#### 4.2 Biological treatments and paths — WRRA interpretation

WRRA connects intervention location, actual change, and functional outcomes. Biological change differs from confirmation of a proposed psychological mechanism.

#### 4.3 Effects of environments and services — WRRA interpretation

External resources and choices are not misrecorded as internal ability. Successful maintenance of function is evaluated with its support conditions.

### 5 Outputs and execution record

The current output is an explanatory specification of phenomena, variables, and validation paths. Planned outputs: effect estimates and tests of mechanisms.

### 6 Falsification conditions and interpretive limits

Falsification condition: revise a mechanism explanation if outcomes improve without change in the designated mechanism.

#### 6.1 Effects, mechanisms, and persistence

Before–after differences alone rarely establish intervention causality. Comparison conditions, natural course, selection, and attrition must be examined. Symptom reduction, functional improvement, burdens, and persistence over time are separated. WRRA mechanism hypotheses receive the same rigor as effect evaluation, and unsupported paths are revised.
