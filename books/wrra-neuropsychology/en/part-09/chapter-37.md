# Chapter 37 Addiction-, Psychosis-, Neurodevelopmental-, and Neurocognitive-Related Phenomena

### 1 Scene and question

These are distinct research targets. Addiction involves difficulties regulating use and its consequences; psychosis involves changes in experience and reality judgment; neurodevelopmental and neurocognitive phenomena involve developmental paths, cognition, and everyday function. Grouping them here does not assume a common cause.

Chapter question: Can a common grammar address different clinical phenomena while preserving mechanistic differences?

### 2 Starting points in established research

#### 2.1 Processes in addiction

Research examines reward, learning, cues, craving, regulation difficulties, and consequences together. Neural–environmental interactions require investigation. (NIDA, n.d. [R29](https://nida.nih.gov/publications/drugs-brains-behavior-science-addiction))

#### 2.2 Psychotic experiences and assessment

Hallucinations, delusions, and changes in thought and activity require assessment alongside suffering, function, and medical conditions. Unfamiliar beliefs alone are not diagnostic; clinical context matters. (NIMH, n.d. [R19](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/topics/schizophrenia))

#### 2.3 Neurodevelopmental differences

ADHD research concerns persistent inattention and hyperactivity–impulsivity symptoms; autism-spectrum research examines varied social-communication and behavioral characteristics. Individuals and support needs vary within each category. (NIMH, n.d. [R30](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/publications/attention-deficit-hyperactivity-disorder-what-you-need-to-know); NIMH, n.d. [R31](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/publications/autism-spectrum-disorder))

#### 2.4 Reading schizophrenia-maintenance evidence by outcome

Ceraso and colleagues reviewed 75 randomized trials with 9,145 participants. In the subset of 30 trials and 4,249 participants comparing relapse at 7–12 months, rates were 24% with medication maintenance and 61% with placebo. The review also evaluated movement-related adverse effects, sedation, and weight gain. (Ceraso et al., 2020 [R63](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD008016.pub3))

Scope of evidence: these are group outcomes within specified follow-up and trial conditions. They do not determine an individual's course or long-term outcomes. Benefits and burdens are separate outputs, a distinction retained in WRRA applications separating symptoms, function, and resources.

### 4 WRRA-specific transformation

Transformation specification: specify different mechanisms and pathways under common questions.

Schizophrenia and other mental disorders require professional assessment and care and cannot be diagnosed with one WRRA residue term. (NIMH, n.d. [R19](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/topics/schizophrenia)) Other conditions likewise require separating current responses, persistent differences, illness and medication, and environmental support. Neurodivergent differences are not all defined as defects to eliminate.

WRRA's shared grammar standardizes questions: What are actual inputs and present states? What influence remains? Where do functional difficulties arise? The mechanisms and values answering them differ by phenomenon. Separate domain models and clinical validation have not yet been performed for this manuscript.

#### 4.1 Processes in addiction — WRRA interpretation

Current inputs, use history, available actions, and costs are recorded separately. Addiction is not defined as one moral or willpower deficiency.

#### 4.2 Psychotic experiences and assessment — WRRA interpretation

WRRA can study input sources, interpretation, confidence, and action connections without identifying causes from one residue value.

#### 4.3 Neurodevelopmental differences — WRRA interpretation

Sensory environments, task demands, strengths, and difficulties are distinguished while person–environment fit is studied.

### 5 Outputs and execution record

The current output is an explanatory specification of phenomena, variables, and validation paths. Planned outputs: phenomenon-specific explanatory models and application scopes.

### 6 Falsification conditions and interpretive limits

Falsification condition: abandon a single-mechanism claim if identical coefficients cannot explain distinct phenomena.

#### 6.1 Neurocognitive function and research scope

Cognitive changes have multiple possible causes, not age alone. Functional trajectories, health, medication, and support require examination. WRRA separates measurement of current state from estimation of persistent change, using different data and rules for different phenomena. This chapter connects research questions; it does not reproduce all conditions with one clinical model.
