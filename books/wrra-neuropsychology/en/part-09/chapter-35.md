# Chapter 35 Anxiety-, Obsession-, and Trauma-Related Phenomena

### 1 Scene and question

Temporary worry differs from persistent anxiety disrupting life. Obsessive-compulsive disorder may involve recurrent unwanted thoughts or excessive repetitive behavior. Post-trauma responses do not take the same form in everyone exposed. (NIMH, n.d. [R14](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/topics/anxiety-disorders); NIMH, n.d. [R15](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/topics/obsessive-compulsive-disorder-ocd); NIMH, n.d. [R16](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/topics/post-traumatic-stress-disorder-ptsd))

Chapter question: How can current danger, experiential residue, and repetitive behavior be distinguished?

### 2 Starting points in established research

#### 2.1 Threat and current danger

Threat anticipation must be examined in relation to present circumstances. Actual high danger differs from persistent reactions under low danger. (NIMH, n.d. [R14](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/topics/anxiety-disorders))

#### 2.2 Obsessive thoughts and compulsive behavior

Content, discomfort, time spent, and functional effects are recorded separately. Distinguishing repetitive behavior's short-term consequences from later course allows maintenance loops to be investigated. (NIMH, n.d. [R15](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/topics/obsessive-compulsive-disorder-ocd))

#### 2.3 Paths after trauma

Responses vary, and many people recover over time. Persistent functional disruption calls for professional assessment. (NIMH, n.d. [R16](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/topics/post-traumatic-stress-disorder-ptsd))

#### 2.4 Placebo-controlled evidence for anxiety-related treatment

Carpenter and colleagues synthesized 41 randomized trials comparing cognitive behavioral therapy with psychological or pill placebo. The combined target-symptom effect was Hedges' g=0.56, varying by diagnosis and analysis population. This standardized between-group difference does not mean 56% of patients recovered. (Carpenter et al., 2018 [R59](https://doi.org/10.1002/da.22728))

Scope of evidence: average effects of treatments containing several procedures differ from the mechanisms transmitting those effects. A WRRA account of expectation or avoidance updating must connect intervention content and actual changes in data.

#### 2.5 Original comparisons of procedures for obsessive-compulsive disorder

Foa and colleagues compared exposure and ritual prevention, clomipramine, their combination, and pill placebo in 122 adult outpatients. At 12 weeks, all three active conditions performed better than placebo. Findings are interpreted within the study's procedures, population, and assessment period. (Foa et al., 2005 [R60](https://doi.org/10.1176/appi.ajp.162.1.151))

Scope of evidence: comparative interventions strengthen discussion separating short-term consequences from later trajectories. Observed effects do not become validation of a particular WRRA residue equation.

#### 2.6 A systematic review of post-traumatic phenomena

Lewis and colleagues reviewed 114 randomized trials of psychological treatments for adult PTSD, involving 8,171 participants. Evidence for trauma-focused CBT, EMDR, and other approaches was evaluated by treatment and comparator. Differences in evidence quantity and quality should not be merged into one psychotherapy effect. (Lewis et al., 2020 [R61](https://doi.org/10.1080/20008198.2020.1729633))

Scope of evidence: residue–avoidance connections are candidate structures for explaining these phenomena. Treatment-effect evidence and direct verification of those candidate connections are recorded separately.

### 4 WRRA-specific transformation

Transformation specification: distinguish current danger, experiential residue, and short-term behavioral consequences.

WRRA separately records threat-related inputs, persisting experiential influences, current danger, and consequences of avoidance or checking. Reduced discomfort after avoidance motivates examining its effect on later choices. Continuing actual danger must not be explained solely by past residue.

#### 4.1 Threat and current danger — WRRA interpretation

External danger, past influences, bodily state, and interpretation are separated. Unpleasant responses are assessed for information and function rather than all being labeled errors.

#### 4.2 Obsessive thoughts and compulsive behavior — WRRA interpretation

WRRA proposes possible explanations without reducing every obsessive-compulsive phenomenon to one reinforcement equation.

#### 4.3 Paths after trauma — WRRA interpretation

Event information, present cues, avoidance, and support are separated. Event severity alone does not determine an individual's later path; continuing current danger must also be distinguished.

### 5 Outputs and execution record

The current output is an explanatory specification of phenomena, variables, and validation paths. Planned outputs: repeated discomfort and choices across situations.

### 6 Falsification conditions and interpretive limits

Falsification condition: responses sufficiently explained by present danger are not classified as maladaptive residue.

Different clinical phenomena are not reduced to one avoidance equation. Behavior may reduce anxiety, reflect habit, or serve another purpose. Exposure or memory manipulations involving people needing treatment are not undertaken on the basis of these hypothetical calculations. This chapter's achievement is specifying research terms and alternative explanations.

#### 6.1 Distinguishing research from intervention

A response-loop model does not establish treatment effectiveness. New interventions require suitable comparisons, risk assessment, and consent. WRRA specifies whether intervention changes input, residue activation, or choice boundaries. Symptoms, function, persistence, and burden must be measured together.
