# Chapter 26 Adolescence and Adulthood

### 1 Scene and question

Adolescence changes roles in school, peer groups, and family, alongside available choices. Adulthood combines work, caregiving, intimacy, and social responsibility in different ways. Biological changes and changing social opportunities must be considered together. (Steinberg, 2008 [R52](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dr.2007.08.002); Spielman et al., 2020, §9.3 [R1](https://openstax.org/books/psychology-2e/pages/9-3-stages-of-development))

Chapter question: How do previous experience and new choices combine in life transitions?

### 2 Starting points in established research

#### 2.1 Development and social transitions

Adolescent and adult change involves school, work, family, and peer expectations alongside biology. People of the same age can have different possible actions and responsibilities. (Spielman et al., 2020, §9.1 [R1](https://openstax.org/books/psychology-2e/pages/9-1-what-is-lifespan-development))

#### 2.2 Risk and available choices

Research on novel choices must examine actual alternatives and failure costs. Giving adults and adolescents the same explanation does not equalize information understanding or social pressure. (Steinberg, 2008 [R52](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dr.2007.08.002))

#### 2.3 Life events and individual differences

Employment changes, parenting, caregiving, and relationship beginnings or endings may not produce changes in the same direction. Timing, expectations, financial support, and personal goals are recorded together. (Spielman et al., 2020, §9.3 [R1](https://openstax.org/books/psychology-2e/pages/9-3-stages-of-development))

### 4 WRRA-specific transformation

Transformation specification: separately update biological conditions and changes in social boundaries.

WRRA also views development as changing boundaries of possible action and relationships. It tracks how existing residue operates as new experiences expand and how changed choice and support alter responses. Brain immaturity alone does not explain every behavior at a given age.

#### 4.1 Development and social transitions — WRRA interpretation

Age is not used merely as one causal variable: changed structures and boundaries are specified. More concrete explanatory units produce clearer testable questions.

#### 4.2 Risk and available choices — WRRA interpretation

Choice computations distinguish present information, experiential residue, and relational expectations. Group means do not automatically classify an individual's judgment.

#### 4.3 Life events and individual differences — WRRA interpretation

WRRA focuses on the concrete inputs and boundaries changing state rather than event names, investigating divergent paths after nominally identical events.

### 5 Outputs and execution record

The current output is an explanatory specification of phenomena, variables, and validation paths. Planned outputs: changes in roles, goals, and choices.

### 6 Falsification conditions and interpretive limits

Falsification condition: limit developmental interpretations if cohort differences explain apparent age effects.

Age-group comparisons can mix cohort and educational differences. Within-person follow-up and group comparisons should be interpreted together. Unemployment or parenting is not treated as an identical stimulus for everyone; income, support, personal meaning, and choice are recorded.

#### 6.1 Stability and persistence of change

Immediate responses to a new role differ from later adaptation. Repeated observations help separate rapid state change from lasting habits. Comparisons with previous generations may mix educational and social differences. WRRA treats within-person change and between-group differences as separate research contracts.
