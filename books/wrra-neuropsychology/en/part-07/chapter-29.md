# Chapter 29 Self-Concept, Identity, and Sense of Agency

### 1 Scene and question

We understand ourselves as particular people and connect action outcomes with our choices. Self-concept includes self-knowledge and evaluation; identity may involve relationships, social roles, and life narratives. Feeling that one initiated an action is a separate research topic. (Haggard, 2017 [R45](https://doi.org/10.1038/nrn.2017.14); Spielman et al., 2020, §9.2 [R1](https://openstax.org/books/psychology-2e/pages/9-2-lifespan-theories))

Chapter question: How do self-description and sense of agency relate to actual controllability?

### 2 Starting points in established research

#### 2.1 Information used to describe the self

Judgments of ability and worth, bodily feelings, social roles, and personal stories contribute to self-understanding. Some information persists while evaluations may change rapidly with context. (Spielman et al., 2020, §9.2 [R1](https://openstax.org/books/psychology-2e/pages/9-2-lifespan-theories))

#### 2.2 Others and self-interpretation

Others' reactions can enter self-evaluation, but their meaning depends on relationships and experience. Receiving criticism differs from interpreting it as a judgment of one's entire worth.

### 4 WRRA-specific transformation

Transformation specification: connect self-related residue, bodily inputs, and relational feedback.

WRRA treats self-related information as currently usable structures. It asks how memory continuity, bodily sensation, and others' responses enter self-interpretation. The whole self is not defined as one storage location or residue value.

#### 4.1 Agency and actual control

Experiencing a choice as one's own and actually being able to change an outcome can be examined separately using altered delays or feedback. WRRA distinguishes possible-action boundaries from reports. Increased perceived control does not alone establish increased external control.

#### 4.2 Information used to describe the self — WRRA interpretation

WRRA separates present states and residues relevant to the question rather than reducing the whole self to one number.

#### 4.3 Others and self-interpretation — WRRA interpretation

The other's behavior, inferred intention, and self-related judgment are distinct terms. Locating change makes intervention hypotheses more concrete.

### 5 Outputs and execution record

The current output is an explanatory specification of phenomena, variables, and validation paths. Planned outputs: changes in self-evaluation, agency, and behavior.

### 6 Falsification conditions and interpretive limits

Falsification condition: changed reports under unchanged control conditions do not establish increased objective control.

Self-reports, behavior, and others' evaluations are observed separately. Subjective agency and objective controllability can also be measured separately. This structure does not claim to resolve philosophical free will; investigation begins with manipulable control conditions and reports.

#### 6.1 Continuity and change

Stories linking past and present selves involve memory and present interpretation. A changed narrative and changed everyday life are checked separately. WRRA treats self-narratives as presently accessible records and tracks their revised interpretations in subsequent action. It does not claim to resolve every philosophical question about the self.
