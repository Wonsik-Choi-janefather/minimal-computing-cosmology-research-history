# Chapter 25 Infancy and Childhood

### 1 Scene and question

Early development combines bodily and neural change, interactions with others, language, and play. Similar ages need not mean identical rates or environments. Developmental stages organize observations rather than placing every individual's changes on one timetable. (Spielman et al., 2020, §9.1 [R1](https://openstax.org/books/psychology-2e/pages/9-1-what-is-lifespan-development))

Chapter question: How can developmental changes be separated from task understanding and measurement differences?

### 2 Starting points in established research

#### 2.1 Developmental domains and interactions

Physical and motor development, perception and language, emotion and social functioning are related but require different observations. The rate in one domain does not determine a child's overall ability. (Spielman et al., 2020, §9.3 [R1](https://openstax.org/books/psychology-2e/pages/9-3-stages-of-development))

#### 2.2 Observing attachment and exploration

Caregiver research can jointly record proximity seeking, exploration, separation, and reunion. Responses in one scene should not be read as lifelong relationship destiny. (Spielman et al., 2020, §9.3 [R1](https://openstax.org/books/psychology-2e/pages/9-3-stages-of-development))

#### 2.3 Developmentally appropriate measurement

Before sufficient verbal responding is possible, gaze, behavior, and play records may be used. Indicator meanings require task-specific validation. Looking longer does not directly establish complete understanding or preference. (Spielman et al., 2020, §9.3 [R1](https://openstax.org/books/psychology-2e/pages/9-3-stages-of-development))

### 4 WRRA-specific transformation

Transformation specification: connect initial conditions, repeated experience, and reciprocal updating.

WRRA asks which residues and relationships repeated interactions form and how they change subsequent exploration and action. Caregiver characteristics and children's responses may influence each other; neither is automatically the sole cause. Bodily growth and task understanding also enter measurement.

#### 4.1 Developmental domains and interactions — WRRA interpretation

WRRA decomposes the structures and resources on which new behavior depends. Increased movement changing exploration and thereby learning is also a researchable connection.

#### 4.2 Observing attachment and exploration — WRRA interpretation

Relational conditions and the child's state are specified, then tendencies persisting across environments are examined.

#### 4.3 Developmentally appropriate measurement — WRRA interpretation

Clear observation–state links allow competing explanations for the same behavior to be compared.

### 6 Falsification conditions and interpretive limits

Falsification condition: avoid direct comparison on one scale if measurement meanings differ across ages.

Adult questions and assumed adult meanings may mismeasure children. Developmentally appropriate indicators include play, gaze, and behavioral observation. This account does not claim that early experience determines lifelong outcomes. Updating under new conditions receives equal research attention.

#### 6.1 Change after early experience

Recognizing early experience's importance also requires studying later environments and support. Persistent difficulties and recovery under new conditions are tracked together. WRRA separates maintenance rates for lasting residue from updating through new experience, connecting repetition and context instead of explaining every later outcome through one event.
