# Chapter 28 Temperament, Personality, and Persistent Individual Differences

### 1 Scene and question

Some people approach unfamiliar situations immediately; others observe first. Understanding these tendencies requires repetition across situations and times. One response may reflect that day's state or a particular relationship. (Spielman et al., 2020, §11.7 [R1](https://openstax.org/books/psychology-2e/pages/11-7-trait-theorists))

Chapter question: How do persistent differences coexist with situation-dependent responses?

### 2 Starting points in established research

#### 2.1 Theories of personality

Trait approaches summarize persistent differences; social-cognitive approaches examine interpretation and learning. Psychodynamic and humanistic perspectives have posed other questions about conflict and self-experience. Historical influence differs from support for individual propositions. (Spielman et al., 2020, §1.2 [R1](https://openstax.org/books/psychology-2e/pages/1-2-history-of-psychology))

#### 2.2 Temperament and personality

Early response tendencies can be studied as temperament without excluding subsequent experience and development. Personality is not assumed to become fixed and manifest identically in every situation. (Spielman et al., 2020, §11.7 [R1](https://openstax.org/books/psychology-2e/pages/11-7-trait-theorists))

#### 2.3 Consistency across situations

Someone may respond differently among friends and under evaluation while retaining repeatable situation-specific tendencies. Repeated observations across situations distinguish individual differences from situational effects. (Spielman et al., 2020, §11.4 [R1](https://openstax.org/books/psychology-2e/pages/11-4-learning-approaches))

### 4 WRRA-specific transformation

Transformation specification: separate persistent traits, states, and environmental selection.

WRRA treats personality as relatively persistent interpretive and response tendencies. It differentiates contributions from initial conditions, accumulated residue, and frequently encountered environments. Existing trait scales provide comparison and measurement material; their names are not automatically translated into neural mechanisms.

#### 4.1 Theories of personality — WRRA interpretation

WRRA specifies which aspects of present state, residue, relationships, and goals a perspective explains.

#### 4.2 Temperament and personality — WRRA interpretation

Separating starting conditions, repeated experiences, and frequently chosen environments supports models permitting both maintenance and change.

#### 4.3 Consistency across situations — WRRA interpretation

Rather than stopping at one coefficient per person, WRRA investigates which conditions activate which rules.

### 6 Falsification conditions and interpretive limits

Falsification condition: revise a single-coefficient interpretation if the same trait has different meanings across situations.

Traits and states are not mutually exclusive. Stable differences allow situational change. Person-specific coefficients require sufficient repeated data; one questionnaire cannot establish every updating rule. Personality types should not become labels restricting human possibilities.

#### 6.1 Uses of personality measurement

Self-reports, others' ratings, and behavior estimate personality through different paths. Disagreement prompts examination of observed situations and periods rather than automatically declaring one wrong. Convenient classifications may inadequately represent complexity. Simplicity in WRRA is judged as minimality preserving actual distinctions.
