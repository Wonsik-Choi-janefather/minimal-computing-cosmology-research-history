# Chapter 19 Language Comprehension, Production, and Acquisition

### 1 Scene and question

The same sentence may be understood differently depending on context. Language research distinguishes sounds and writing, word meaning, sentence structure, and conversational context. Understanding language and expressing one's meaning are not fully revealed by the same test. (Spielman et al., 2020, §7.2 [R1](https://openstax.org/books/psychology-2e/pages/7-2-language))

Chapter question: Which processes are shared or different in comprehension and production?

### 2 Starting points in established research

#### 2.1 Levels of language

Speech-sound discrimination, word forms and meanings, sentence structure, and utterance intentions can be distinguished. The same words may convey different intentions depending on the interlocutor and preceding context. (Spielman et al., 2020, §7.2 [R1](https://openstax.org/books/psychology-2e/pages/7-2-language))

#### 2.2 Observing comprehension and production

Reading time, comprehension accuracy, speech-onset time, and speech errors are different indicators. Slow word retrieval must be distinguished from failure to understand a sentence.

#### 2.3 Development and multilingual environments

Introductory accounts of language units and development treat acquisition as a topic in cognitive development. (Spielman et al., 2020, §7.2 [R1](https://openstax.org/books/psychology-2e/pages/7-2-language))

### 4 WRRA-specific transformation

Transformation specification: connect current language knowledge and conversational residue to interpretation rules.

Auditory or visual language signals are inputs. Currently available vocabulary, grammatical knowledge, and conversational traces provide interpretive conditions. Outcomes are observed as comprehension judgments or speech. Response paths must be separated so that not speaking is not equated with not understanding.

#### 4.1 Computing conversational residue

A possible task tests how a referent in the preceding sentence influences a following pronoun's interpretation. Candidate referents, relationships, and context-retention rates could define a small model. No numerical execution was performed here, so this remains a design. Later inputs of actual interpretations and times across contexts could assess the required maintenance rule.

#### 4.2 Levels of language — WRRA interpretation

WRRA distinguishes inputs and relationships at each level and specifies transition rules between them. Language processing is not reduced to one word lookup.

#### 4.3 Observing comprehension and production — WRRA interpretation

Task-specific output functions allow comparison of how the same experiential residue appears in different tests, making access and expression paths concrete.

#### 4.4 Development and multilingual environments — WRRA interpretation

Proposed studies record input quantity and quality, conversational opportunities, and contexts of use alongside age. Comparisons involving bilingual speakers specify which language was used in which situations. The book's interpretive principle is that one test under different language histories should not be translated into differences in general intelligence or personal worth.

### 5 Outputs and execution record

The current output is an explanatory specification of phenomena, variables, and validation paths. Planned outputs: comprehension accuracy, speech, and reaction time.

### 6 Falsification conditions and interpretive limits

Falsification condition: outcomes explained by response-mode differences do not establish comprehension-ability differences.

Explaining acquisition requires specifying developmental timing, languages encountered, and interaction types and quantities. This book does not derive all language principles from one residue equation. It proposes first computing preceding-context effects in short conversations and testing changed interpretations when context changes.
