# Chapter 50 Consciousness, Entropy, and Time


### 1 Scene and Question

Ten clock minutes can feel long on one occasion and short on another. More complex neural signals during wakefulness do not make their numerical complexity identical to the universe's thermodynamic entropy. How can experienced and physical time, informational uncertainty, and energy costs be distinguished while studying their connections?

### 2 The Starting Point in Existing Research

Thermodynamic entropy concerns physical states under specified conditions. Shannon entropy measures uncertainty in a declared probability distribution. Neural signal complexity depends on sampling, preprocessing, and estimation. Mathematical connections may exist, but units and measurement models cannot be omitted and the quantities treated as interchangeable. A brain exchanging energy with its environment does not by itself determine an entropy value for a particular experience.

Carhart-Harris and colleagues (2014) proposed a theory connecting conscious states with neural entropy, informed by neuroimaging research with psychedelic drugs. It is read as a hypothesis relating contents and signal characteristics. Increasing random noise cannot simply mean richer or higher consciousness, and signal entropy does not establish a cosmic purpose. [R85]

Landauer (1961) examined the relationship between logically irreversible information processing and heat generation. This does not establish a fixed energy cost for every brain operation or identify one memory bit with one unit of experience. Actual device costs, lower bounds, thermodynamic conditions, and logical operations must be distinguished. [R86]

### 3 Verification Inputs

Timing tasks separately record physical stimulus intervals, attention conditions, memory content, arousal, and retrospective duration reports. Entropy analysis requires signal units, sampling rate, window length, estimator, and noise model. Energy claims require physical measurement or a declared cost model. If current memory and later recollection are measured at different times, that distinction remains in the inputs.

### 4 WRRA-Specific Transformation

The author's earlier time essay analyzed experienced time from the position that past effects must be presently instantiated as memory, state, or records. This is the author's philosophical and modeling proposal, not experimental proof that only the present exists. Difficulty attributing experienced duration without consciousness also differs from claiming that physical change or clock time disappears. [W5]

The Core 2.1 application distinguishes physical interval Δt from reported duration T_report. Render_D must specify how G maintenance and comparison rules, current attention and activation X, retained traces R, and constraints B generate reports. An anticipated future enters as a present prediction state; the actual future is not assumed already present in the ledger. [W3, W5]

A proposed experiment changes attention or retention while holding physical interval constant, then compares duration reports. Claiming both reduced operation counts and reduced energy requires a separate operation-to-physical-cost mapping. Maintaining organization may cost more while task processing costs less, so minimum computation does not mean every cost decreases simultaneously. Appropriate functions remain to be determined from data; no arbitrary-coefficient law of time perception is invented here.
