# Chapter 49 How Might Consciousness Arise and Persist, and How Can It Be Assessed?


### 1 Scene and Question

The same person stops responding under anesthesia and later responds again. Their body and all long-term memories have not disappeared and been recreated. What changed temporarily, and what remained? This chapter separates onset, persistence, interruption, and recovery into organization and execution conditions, then locates measurements within those distinctions.

### 2 The Starting Point in Existing Research

Standardized behavioral measures such as the CRS-R organize observations under clinical conditions. Repeated assessments and confounder checks matter; a total score should not be read as the subjective richness of any possible entity. PCI calculates complexity of TMS-evoked EEG responses under a defined procedure. These approaches have different inputs, measurements, and interpretations, rather than being measurements of one quantity in a shared unit. [R74, R78]

Multidimensional accounts of animal consciousness also caution against compressing every difference into a single level. How strong the evidence for experience is and how rich particular characteristics might be are separate axes. This distinction is the book's reporting rule, informed by R81's multidimensional approach. [R81]

### 3 Verification Inputs

Inputs include trial-level stimuli, reports, behavior, neural responses, transition times, medication and sleep conditions, and structural damage and recovery records. Resources B retain their units, such as time, memory, or energy; subjective fatigue is not automatically entered as the same quantity. Unmeasured resources are marked hypothetical. No new patient dataset or neural simulation of consciousness onset is included.

### 4 WRRA-Specific Transformation

#### 4.1 Onset, maintenance, interruption, and recovery

Onset concerns a candidate executable organization and experience-related observations becoming established from declared initial conditions. Maintenance concerns continuing its functions under allowed perturbations and costs. Interruption distinguishes stopping execution, access, or report; recovery identifies what returns using retained G, R, and records. Bodily persistence alone does not establish continuous experience, and interrupted reporting does not establish structural destruction.

Inheritance is recorded as G_(k+1)=Inherit_D(G_k, amendment_k, retained_state_k). Declare the analysis unit and owner, carrier, inherited components, permissible changes, reconstruction procedure, and compatibility checks. This is a Core 2.1 interface, not an equation establishing a single brain mechanism. Whether a memory change is treated as a state change or structural change depends on the declared timescale and task. [W3]

#### 4.2 Resources and maintenance

The Core condition B_j−Cbound_j(a)−Hbound_j(a)≥Reserve_j includes bounds on action costs and checking, recording, and response costs, together with reserves. It specifies execution authorization, not a consciousness threshold. Connecting resource limitations to consciousness maintenance requires application rules and observations specifying which function deteriorates first. Costs of bodily maintenance and additional experience-related processing must not be counted twice. [W3]

#### 4.3 Report two assessment axes

The first axis concerns evidence for experience: test conditions, positive and negative findings, sensory and motor pathways, alternatives, and evidence types. Unvalidated weights must not generate numerical claims such as an 80% probability of consciousness. The second axis describes characteristics such as perceptual discrimination, value-sensitive choices, temporal retention, and self-related access. Measurement comparability across humans, animals, and artificial systems requires separate assessment.

### 5 Outputs and Execution Record

The completed outputs are transition specifications and a two-axis reporting procedure. Subsequent studies could measure retention across delays, access-recovery times after perturbation, and performance changes after removing connections. These measure declared functions, not experience in isolation. Clinical validity and cross-species measurement validity of new indicators were not established in this edition.

### 6 Falsification Conditions and Interpretive Boundaries

If a function persists without an organization claimed necessary for maintenance, revise the necessity claim. A recovery model that classifies states but fails on intervention outcomes is incomplete as a control model. If reproduction requires removing resource constraints, implementation under finite resources has not been established. Functional persistence and continuity of the same subject remain separate issues.

### 7 Current Verdict

The Core 2.1 onset, maintenance, inheritance, and reporting specifications are INFERRED. Sufficient conditions for experience, a universal consciousness scale, and clinical control effects remain OPEN. The resource gate is not used as a consciousness detector.
