# Chapter 47 Are Animals Conscious?


### 1 Scene and Question

An animal avoids a harmful stimulus. This could be a reflex, or an unpleasant experience could influence its next choice. How can experience be inferred without language report, and how should species with different characteristics be compared? Similarity to humans alone does not place all species on one consciousness ladder.

### 2 The Starting Point in Existing Research

Birch, Schnell, and Clayton (2020) discussed perceptual richness, evaluative richness, integration at a time, integration across time, and self-consciousness as dimensions of animal consciousness. One species need not rank above another on every dimension. Sensory ecology and task demands differ, so test failure must be separated from a mismatch between the test and the animal. This multidimensional approach is credited to those researchers, not claimed as an original WRRA invention. [R81]

Crook (2021) combined injury-related place avoidance, preference associated with analgesic treatment, and neurophysiological evidence in octopuses to support affective pain experience. The significance is converging observations rather than simply identifying one avoidance response with pain. The findings are not generalized to all invertebrates or all harmful stimuli. Nociception processes damage-related signals; it is not definitionally identical to an unpleasant pain experience. [R82]

The 2024 New York Declaration on Animal Consciousness described strong scientific support for conscious experience in mammals and birds and reasons to consider its possibility in other animal groups. This is an expert judgment about the evidence, not one controlled experiment or a definitive judgment on every species. Fish, cephalopods, and insects require attention to evidence for the particular species and tasks. [R83]

### 3 Verification Inputs

Inputs specify species, developmental stage, ecologically appropriate sensory capabilities, stimulus intensity, changing avoidance and preferences, persistence of learning, motivational trade-offs, and physiological signals. Motor incapacity and unsuitable stimuli must be controlled. Treatments may affect learning or movement independently of pain. This book contains no new animal experiments; these are specifications for subsequent testing.

### 4 WRRA-Specific Transformation

Declare the individual as the analysis unit. G specifies organization connecting sensation, retention, evaluation, and action selection; X contains present activation and motivational state; R contains current effects of injury and experience. Resources in B are identified as measured or hypothetical and retain their units. A persistent preference does not alone determine whether its basis is an R retention rule or a learned change in G, so competing rules require comparison. [W3]

The comparison concerns what distinctions an organization preserves and uses in subsequent choices under the task contract, rather than neuron count alone. A function successfully generating pain-related behavior is an achievement of behavioral modeling, not sole proof of pain experience. Reporting strength of evidence for experience separately from a profile of functional characteristics prevents limited human-like language from erasing other evidence.

### 5 Outputs and Execution Record

Planned outputs are species-specific profiles and tables of evidence supporting or weakening particular experience hypotheses. Temporal integration might be investigated through persistence of effects under delays, and evaluative characteristics through choices between competing objectives. These are task-specific operational indicators, not a universal score obtained by arbitrarily adding quantities with different units. The completed output is the comparison procedure.

### 6 Falsification Conditions and Interpretive Boundaries

A model claiming flexible evaluation must be revised if it cannot reproduce conditions distinguishing its rule from a reflex alternative. If a failure reflects sensory or motor conditions, it should not count as evidence of absent consciousness. Evidence can accumulate, but uncertainty remains species-specific. Measurement uncertainty and value judgments about animal treatment are also different questions.

### 7 Current Verdict

Evidence supporting experience in some animals is acknowledged within the scope of the cited research and declaration. WRRA mappings across species are INFERRED; their empirical advantages and extension to nonbiological systems remain OPEN. No single consciousness grade is assigned to all animals.
