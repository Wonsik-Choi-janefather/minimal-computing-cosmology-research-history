# Chapter 13 Conditioning and Reinforcement

### 1 Scene and question

An animal responding to a sound previously followed by food differs from behavior changing after an action is rewarded. The first centers on relationships between stimuli; the second on relationships between action and consequence. Reinforcement and punishment must be defined by actual behavioral change. (Spielman et al., 2020, §6.2 [R1](https://openstax.org/books/psychology-2e/pages/6-2-classical-conditioning); §6.3 [R1](https://openstax.org/books/psychology-2e/pages/6-3-operant-conditioning))

Chapter question: How do stimuli, actions, and consequences on earlier trials change the next response?

### 2 Starting points in established research

#### 2.1 Learning relationships between stimuli

Classical conditioning concerns not merely co-occurrence but the information one stimulus provides about another event. Timing, event probabilities, and prior learning can be distinguished. (Spielman et al., 2020, §6.2 [R1](https://openstax.org/books/psychology-2e/pages/6-2-classical-conditioning))

#### 2.2 Four kinds of behavioral consequence

Reinforcement is a relationship in which subsequent consequences increase behavior; punishment decreases it. Positive and negative mean adding and removing a stimulus. Negative reinforcement is therefore not another name for bad punishment. If an action stopping an unpleasant sound increases, it can be analyzed as reinforcement by removal. (Spielman et al., 2020, §6.3 [R1](https://openstax.org/books/psychology-2e/pages/6-3-operant-conditioning))

#### 2.3 Extinction and context

Researchers examine decreasing responses when learned consequences are no longer provided. Whether the decrease means complete deletion of the earlier relationship requires tests after delays or in other contexts. (Spielman et al., 2020, §6.2 [R1](https://openstax.org/books/psychology-2e/pages/6-2-classical-conditioning))

#### 2.4 Evidence distinguishing extinction from complete deletion

Bouton's review compares the return of extinguished responses after changes in time or context in Pavlovian extinction research. Such findings support context-dependent performance after extinction and challenge a simple reversal-of-learning account. This is a review of behavioral research, not a direct measurement of persisting memory molecules. (Bouton, 2004 [R56](https://doi.org/10.1101/lm.78804))

Scope of evidence: this book examines explanations separating maintained states and current expression. A claim that residue actually remains requires comparing behavioral evidence under return conditions with candidate maintenance rules.

### 4 WRRA-specific transformation

Transformation specification: update residue from earlier relationships according to outcomes.

Changes left by earlier trials act in the next trial's present. Updating based on the difference between expected and actual outcomes is one possible implementation. Its rule must be specified and computed within WRRA; citing an established learning model does not complete implementation.

#### 4.1 Existing learning equations and WRRA

An existing expectation–outcome difference rule can be implemented within an input–residue–output structure. Its original contribution is credited while the boundaries and observations connected through WRRA are recorded. Actual execution within WRRA counts as computational performance even if the same numbers result. Merely citing an equation does not establish integration.

#### 4.2 Learning relationships between stimuli — WRRA interpretation

Relationships are specified as states remaining active now. Naming sound and food is insufficient; the update changing the next response must be determined.

#### 4.3 Four kinds of behavioral consequence — WRRA interpretation

#### 4.4 Extinction and context — WRRA interpretation

WRRA distinguishes maintained residue from current expression when designing tests. Conditions are specified to discriminate maintenance hypotheses rather than explaining every possible outcome through hidden memory.

### 5 Outputs and execution record

The current output is an explanatory specification of phenomena, variables, and validation paths. Planned outputs: choice and response on the next trial.

### 6 Falsification conditions and interpretive limits

Falsification condition: narrow a claim of different learning amounts if current performance conditions explain the difference.

Faster responses in a more-rewarded group do not alone establish more learning. Satiety, motor capacity, and task comprehension may differ. Separating learning residue from current expression conditions distinguishes what was learned from what is displayed now.
