#### 4.1 Label data roles

Distinguish data seen during construction, used to determine coefficients, and evaluated after fixing rules. Multiple roles for the same data must be disclosed. Reproducing known findings can be an independent purpose. Reporting that achievement separately from new-condition performance avoids treating calibration itself as improper.

#### 4.2 Decide what to compare

Scope, output error, data requirements, computational cost, and interpretability are different measures. WRRA need not always outperform every alternative to contribute meaningfully. Assessment asks which structural connection serves which purpose. Existing models receive the same inputs and error criteria, without credit for mechanisms they did not implement.

### 6 Falsification conditions and interpretive limits

Falsification condition: revise or reject the claim after repeated mismatches outside prespecified tolerances.

Falsification conditions must not move after results are seen. Tolerances reflect measurement error and goals and are specified beforehand; failures are recorded. Failure of a residue rule supports rejecting that rule. Rejecting every Core application simultaneously requires separate argument. Comparison models receive the same standard.

#### 6.1 Distinguish structural and coefficient falsification

Wrong magnitudes, opposite condition effects, and failure to reproduce historical influences under the same present conditions call for different revisions. Coefficient calibration differs from changing the state distinctions themselves. Neither success nor failure is converted into a verdict on the whole Core beyond the claim's scope.

#### 6.2 Preserve revision history

Revising hypotheses and code is normal research. Earlier inputs, rules, and failures are retained and changed terms identified. Repeated fitting to the same data is not described as a first evaluation after freezing. WRRA's explanatory and predictive ownership identifies its own computations while accurately crediting other theories.

#### 6.3 Failures determine the next design

If residue is unhelpful, current measurements may already contain enough historical influence. Redundancy of one residue term differs from all residue being unnecessary. Distinguishing observation and model turns failures into new questions: this is the research role of fail-closed practice.
