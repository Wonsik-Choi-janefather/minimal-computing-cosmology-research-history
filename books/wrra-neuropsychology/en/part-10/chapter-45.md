#### 4.2 Following one scene through to completion

Recall Chapter 1's person who stops speaking before a presentation. Connect that scene to Chapter 42's hypothetical computation. This is not an analysis of actual participants. It is an explanatory application reading challenge choice as choosing to speak again when an opportunity is provided. Suitability for actual presentations remains untested.

**Starting from existing explanations**

Established distinctions separate earlier experience, current performance conditions, and actual behavior. Chapter 14 distinguishes acquisition from current performance; Chapter 15 distinguishes trace retention from access. Chapter 21 does not substitute goals and intentions for all behavior. These distinctions were needed before introducing WRRA. Its role is to show where each enters a scene's computation, not claim them as new discoveries.

The explanation “earlier failure prevented speaking” still leaves gaps. What counted as failure? How much influence remains until the next opportunity? How do current conditions enter? Were having no speaking opportunity and remaining silent despite an opportunity counted identically? Without these specifications, different explanations may label the same silence without showing computationally how they differ.

**Turning explanation into an execution specification**

Chapter 42 externally supplies failure counts. Their retained influence is residue, updated as 0.8 times the preceding residue plus 0.4 for a new failure, starting from 0. Current conditions use dimensionless resources 1.0 and 0.3. Available actions and speaking opportunities are assumed equal.

The output is challenge-choice probability, not speech content or success. Chapter 42 adds 0.9 times resources to baseline 1.2, subtracts 1.1 times residue, and applies the logistic function. These are hypothetical coefficients showing separate entry points for residue and present conditions, not estimates from presentation research. The function is unchanged from Chapter 42 and Appendix D.

**Reading the executed values**

Before failure, probabilities are 0.890903 at resources 1.0 and 0.813057 at 0.3. Five failures produce residue 1.344640 and probabilities 0.650422 and 0.497724. Equal residue yields different outputs under different current resources. Within this model, past influence alone cannot determine current choice probability.

Stopping new failures and applying five maintenance updates yields residue 0.440612 and probability 0.834150 at resources 1.0. Neither successful experience nor counseling was included. This is the specified maintenance rule under no additional failure, not a recovery rate for presentation anxiety or a treatment effect of support.

**Questions revealed by the connecting specification**

First, silence counts alone cannot determine whether differences reflect earlier experience or present conditions. Requiring separate inputs reveals additional records needed. Second, resources change outputs but remain unmapped to sleep duration, preparation time, or support quantity. Real application must identify the chosen condition and its numerical mapping.

Third, without guaranteed opportunities the current boundary assumption fails: opportunity availability must first be recorded, then choice conditional on an opportunity analyzed. Fourth, choice probabilities do not establish absolute residue magnitude. Doubling residue scale while halving its output coefficient preserves probabilities in Chapter 42. Identical outputs therefore do not guarantee a unique account of internal state.

The explanatory contribution is a single specification distinguishing observed actions, past influence, present conditions, and choice boundaries, while tracing which assumptions change which outputs. It is a constructive achievement preserving established distinctions and revealing gaps in execution and interpretation. It is neither a new psychological law nor evidence of better fit than existing models.

**What remains unconnected**

The computation contains no neural activity or bodily responses. It does not specify residue's circuit or physiological implementation, or how reports relate to model values. Action-dependent changes in subsequent failures are also unimplemented. These lead to Chapter 43's observation paths and temporal alignment. What is complete is choice computation under declared hypothetical conditions; empirical behavioral–neural connections remain future research.
